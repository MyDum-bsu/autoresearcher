## 1. The subject of the project

Two-stage adaptive robust optimization (ARO) with fixed recourse, and
its bridge to guaranteed optimal control (Dmitruk school): a linear
discrete system driven by scalar control and a disturbance
w ∈ {−1, +1}^T, terminal cost ‖x_T‖₁, where one may schedule closure
moments R (instants at which the state is measured and control is
re-adjusted). The bridge question: how the guaranteed value V(R)
behaves as R varies, and what that says about when simple (affine)
decision rules are exact in two-stage ARO.

## 5. Knowledge base (literature program, started 2026-10-11)

Processed 13 papers, 107 blocks registered (verified anchors):
Tsang-Shehadeh-Curtis 2022, Bertsimas-Shtern 2018, Xu-Burer 2017,
BGT-2004, and (blocks from) the bridge theorems. Load-bearing findings:

- **BGT-2004 (the founding paper) explicitly does NOT claim AARC
  exactness**: AARC is introduced as "the simplest restriction";
  exactness holds only in degenerate cases (Thm 2.1 tight conditions,
  Thm 2.2 vertex reformulation of ARC — nothing about affine rules);
  the only quantitative benchmark (Thm 4.3, factor <= 6) bounds the SDP
  surrogate, not the AARC-vs-ARC gap; the empirical 3.4% closeness is
  observed, not proven. Our conj-ldr-exactness targets a gap the
  founding paper leaves open — confirmed from the source.
- **Xu-Burer 2017** gives the exactness gap its precise published
  language: the two-stage problem with RHS uncertainty IS a copositive
  program (exactly), the affine policy is the IA(Q) inner-cone rung,
  and the gap is a cone-membership question; the SOC temporal-network
  family (affine gap (sqrt(s)+s)/2 vs s, closed exactly by SDP) is the
  mandatory negative test for any exactness condition we formulate.
- **Bertsimas-Shtern 2018**: the dual wedge W basis partition
  (piecewise linear, x-independent dual policy — dual-side exactness),
  the complementarity-oracle breakdown under mere feasibility (CCG
  infeasible on 31–34% of instances), and the authors' own open
  question of when affine rules are optimal.
- **Tsang-Shehadeh-Curtis 2022**: i-C&CG multiplicative error-budget
  certificates — methods template for our certified pipelines;
  orthogonal to the decision-rule line (registered as contrast).
- **GTW-2020 (primal-dual lifting)**: a second, mechanism-distinct
  exactness lever — the barycentric piecewise-affine completion
  (Proposition 5) is exact under affine T, h and FIXED recourse over any
  bounded polyhedral Xi with NO M-matrix or index-set assumptions
  (Proposition 1); provably incomparable with the 2025 condition (B) —
  our exactness taxonomy must state which lever each condition uses.
- **Zeng-Zhao 2013**: the C&CG foundation our contrast rests on
  (O(p) convergence, unified optimality/feasibility cuts, master
  strength ordering); its assumptions inventory is the checklist our
  exactness conditions sharpen; the convergence proofs live in an
  Electronic Companion not in our corpus [requires verification].
- **Dmitruk school line A1-A3 (2021-2024 prehistory of A4/A5)**: the
  schedule is an INPUT in all three (A1 chooses it by hand; A3 fixes
  the closing instant a priori), the school itself declares the optimal
  closure moment OPEN (A2, p. 681), and A3 explicitly flags the
  piecewise-affine structure of f_j(alpha) under a rank condition as
  the unclaimed thread our scheduling law occupies. Polarity discipline:
  A1 is max-min, A2/A3/A4 flip to min-max cost - our ladder statements
  must state the polarity explicitly. A2 Statement 3: a zero-information
  certificate (one active row => zero closure value) - a benchmark for
  our wedge-side zero-value cases.

Also processed: GTW-2025 (condition (B) extracted verbatim; (B) is
NOT necessary - our 10/18 quantification is a one-sided witness, not a
gap proof) and Dmitruk A4 (the closure schedule is FIXED a priori there
- collision gate G1 passed; the ladder refinement monotonicity is not
