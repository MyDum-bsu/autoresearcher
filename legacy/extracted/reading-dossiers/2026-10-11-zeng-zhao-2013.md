## Theorem blocks

### thm-zz-1: Exact reformulation for finite U and partial-enumeration relaxation
- statement: If U = {u_1, ..., u_r} is finite, introducing per-scenario recourse variables x^l turns the two-stage RO (1) into an equivalent monolithic MIP (5)–(9): min c'y + η s.t. Ay ≥ d; η ≥ b'x^l; Ey + Gx^l ≥ h − Mu_l; y ∈ S_y, x^l ∈ S_x, l = 1,...,r. For a large or polyhedral U this enumeration is intractable, but a formulation defined over a subset of U is a valid relaxation, hence a lower bound; growing the subset strengthens the bound.
- conditions: Both stages linear; U finite discrete for the equivalence; y, x may be discrete or continuous (so the equivalent program is "probably large-scale mixed integer").
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "As a result, solving a two-stage RO problem reduces to solve an equivalent (probably large-scale) mixed integer program."
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "a formulation based on a partial enumeration, i.e., a formulation defined over a subset of U, provides a valid relaxation (and, consequently, a lower bound) to the original two-stage RO (or its equivalent formulation)."
- our-relevance: This is the primal-side foundation of C&CG and also the shape of any scenario-truncated lower bound we might certify in a pipeline with staged (scheduled) scenario admission.

### thm-zz-2: Finite convergence of the Benders-dual method — O(pq) (Proposition 1)
- statement: Let p be the number of extreme points of U (if U is a polyhedron) or the cardinality of U (if discrete), and q the number of extreme points of {π : G'π ≤ b, π ≥ 0}. Then the Benders-dual algorithm generates an optimal solution of (1) in O(pq) iterations.
- conditions: Second-stage problem is an LP in x; the relatively complete recourse assumption ("this LP is feasible for any given y and u", p. 2); a solution oracle returns an optimal (u*_k, π*_k) of the bilinear subproblem Q(y) (2); dual cuts η ≥ (h − Ey − Mu*_k)'π*_k (3) accumulate in master MP1 (4). Proof: Electronic Companion A1 `[requires verification — EC not in corpus]`.
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "Then, the Benders-dual algorithm will generate an optimal solution to (1) in O(pq) iterations."
- our-relevance: The baseline complexity bound for duality-based cutting planes; any exactness theorem we state for our pipelines should be compared against this O(pq) counting argument.

### thm-zz-3: Finite convergence of C&CG — O(p) (Proposition 2)
- statement: With p as in thm-zz-2, the C&CG algorithm converges to the optimal value of (1) in O(p) iterations. The iteration count is thus smaller than Benders-dual's by a factor O(q) when the second stage is an LP.
- conditions: U a polyhedron (count extreme points) or a finite discrete set (count elements); an oracle for SP2 (10) that either returns an optimal (u*, x*) with finite Q(y) or identifies some u* ∈ U with infeasible second stage (Q(y) = +∞ by convention); master MP2 (11) carries η ≥ b'x^l for l ∈ O (optimality variables) and Ey + Gx^l ≥ h − Mu*_l for all identified scenarios l ≤ k; stopping test UB − LB ≤ ε. The explicit finite-termination-with-proof claim is made for an LP second stage under relatively complete recourse (see thm-zz-4); proof: Electronic Companion A2 `[requires verification — EC not in corpus]`.
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "It can either derive an optimal solution (u∗, x∗) with a finite optimal value Q(y) or identify some u∗ ∈ U for which the second-stage decision problem is infeasible. Q(y) in the latter case is set to +∞ by convention."
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 3 — "the C&CG algorithm will converge to the optimal value of (1) in O(p) iterations."
- our-relevance: This is the central exactness result of our line 1 (master/subproblem structure + finite convergence); the assumptions inventory (polytope U, oracle, LP + relatively complete recourse for termination) is exactly the checklist our own exactness conditions must sharpen.

### thm-zz-4: Unified optimality/feasibility cuts and finite termination (LP + relatively complete recourse)
- statement: Cuts (12)–(13) added when Q(y*_{k+1}) < +∞ serve as optimality cuts; constraint (14) added when Q(y*_{k+1}) = +∞ serves as a feasibility cut. Since (12) remains valid even for an infeasible scenario, both (12)–(13) can be generated for any identified scenario — one unified cut mechanism. If the second-stage problem is an LP and relatively complete recourse holds, the algorithm terminates in finitely many iterations.
- conditions: LP second stage; relatively complete recourse for the termination clause; infeasible scenarios witnessed by the oracle's identified u*_{k+1}. Proof of termination: Electronic Companion A2 `[requires verification — EC not in corpus]`.
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 3 — "Note that constraints (12)–(13) generated in Step 4(a) serve as optimality cuts and constraints (14) generated in Step 4(b) serve as feasibility cuts. In fact, because constraint (12) with x^{k+1} for an infeasible scenario is also valid, we can simply generate both (12) and (13) for any identified scenario."
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 3 — "Next, if the second-stage problem is LP and the relatively complete recourse assumption holds, this algorithm terminates in a finite number of iterations (see A2 in the Electronic Companion [11] for the proof)."
- our-relevance: The primal-cut template our certified pipelines imitate: a single constraint family carrying both optimality and feasibility, with the feasibility witness supplied by the adversary solve.

### thm-zz-5: Master-problem strength ordering (Proposition 3)
- statement: For the same set of identified scenarios {u*_1, ..., u*_k} appearing in both masters, the optimal objective of MP1 (Benders-dual master) is an underestimation of that of MP2 (C&CG master).
- conditions: Relatively complete recourse assumption (invoked in discussion point (v)); identical scenario set in both masters. Proof: Electronic Companion A3 `[requires verification — EC not in corpus]`.
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 3 — "For the same set of scenarios u∗1, u∗2, . . . , u∗k that are considered in both of the master problems, the objective function of MP1 is an underestimation of that of MP2."
- our-relevance: A cheap certified invariant (dual-side master value ≤ primal-side master value on equal scenarios) that our judges could check mechanically at every iteration of a C&CG-style loop.

### thm-zz-6: KKT / big-M exact reformulation of the adversarial subproblem for polyhedral U
- statement: Under relatively complete recourse, SP2 (max_u min_x b'x) is equivalent to the KKT system (15)–(20): max b'x s.t. Gx ≥ h − Ey − Mu; G'π ≤ b; complementarity (Gx − h + Ey + Mu)_i π_i = 0 ∀i and (b − G'π)_j x_j = 0 ∀j; u ∈ U, x ∈ S_x, π ≥ 0. Linearizing complementarity with binaries v_j and big-M (x_j ≤ Mv_j, (b − G'π)_j ≤ M(1 − v_j)) converts SP2 into a 0–1 MIP solvable by existing solvers.
- conditions: Relatively complete recourse; LP second stage; quality of the formulation depends on tight analytic bounds on M (paper points to Gabrel et al. [13] for the robust location-transportation case).
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 3 — "Constraints in (18) and (19) are complementary slackness conditions, where i and j are appropriate indices for variables or constraints. By making use of the big-M method, they can be linearized by introducing binary variables."
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 3 — "So, SP2 can be converted into a 0–1 mixed integer program and computed by an existing solver. We recognize that if a tight bound on big-M can be analytically obtained, e.g., the study in [13] on the robust location-transportation problem, a better performance can be achieved."
- our-relevance: This is the paper's optimality certificate for the adversary (duality-based, as in our line 3); the big-M tightness requirement is a concrete gate-artifact pattern — derive M bounds symbolically and freeze them as path+sha256.

## Fact blocks

### fact-zz-1: Problem setting of the letter
- Both stages are linear optimization models; U is either a finite discrete set or a polyhedron; y (first stage) and x (second stage) may each take discrete or continuous values. General form: min_y c'y + max_{u∈U} min_{x∈z(y,u)} b'x s.t. Ay ≥ d, y ∈ S_y, with z(y,u) = {x ∈ S_x : Gx ≥ h − Ey − Mu}. The Benders-dual line of methods applies only when S_x = R^m_+ (LP recourse).
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "where z(y, u) = {x ∈ Sx : Gx ≥ h − Ey − Mu} with Sy ⊆ Rn+ and Sx ⊆ Rm+ . A few cutting plane based methods have been developed and implemented to derive the exact solution when Sx = Rm+"
- our-relevance: Fixes the model class for which C&CG is claimed exact; integer recourse is handled only via the nested extension of fact-zz-4.

### fact-zz-2: Benders-dual subproblem is bilinear; bound bookkeeping
- The Benders-dual subproblem SP1, Q(y) = max{(h − Ey − Mu)'π : G'π ≤ b, u ∈ U, π ≥ 0}, is a bilinear optimization problem (heuristics or specially structured U were the prior remedies). With an oracle optimal (u*_k, π*_k), cut (3) is added to MP1; c'y*_k + Q(y*_k) is an upper bound and c'y*_{k+1} + η*_{k+1} a lower bound on the optimum of (1); moreover π*_k and u*_k are extreme points (or discrete points) of their respective feasible sets — which drives the O(pq) count.
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "Note that the resulting problem in (2) is a bilinear optimization problem."
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "cT y∗k + Q(y∗k) provides an upper bound and cT y∗k+1 + η∗k+1 provides a lower bound to the optimal value of (1)."
- our-relevance: Our certified pipelines reuse exactly this bound pair; the bilinearity is the structural obstacle that our own oracle designs must break without losing certification.

### fact-zz-3: Feasibility-cut gap for the Benders-dual line
- Without relatively complete recourse, the letter states that Benders-dual feasibility cuts are problem-specific: Terry [18] (PhD thesis, 2009) and Jiang et al. [15] (tech report, 2011) discuss feasibility cut schemes; C&CG is advertised as a general treatment (difference (ii)).
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 2 — "In the cases where the relatively complete recourse assumption does not hold, Terry [18] and Jiang et al. [15] discuss the feasibility cut issue."
- our-relevance: Delimits the assumption footprint of thm-zz-2/3/4 — outside relatively complete recourse the letter offers no certificate, which is where our own exactness conditions could contribute.

### fact-zz-4: Solution capability beyond LP recourse
- Difference (iv): Benders-dual requires an LP second stage; C&CG is indifferent to variable types in the second stage, and the authors extended it in a nested fashion to two-stage RO with mixed-integer recourse (Zhao & Zeng, tech report 2012, ref [20]).
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 3 — "Different from the Benders-dual algorithm, which requires the second-stage problem to be an LP problem, the C&CG algorithm is indifferent to the variable types in the second stage."
- our-relevance: Relevant to our BGT-2004-class production-inventory connection whenever first-stage/second-stage variables are integer; note the finite-convergence theorems are nonetheless stated for the LP case.

### fact-zz-5: Computational evidence (location-transportation case study)
- Case study: two-stage robust location-transportation problem (22)–(26) with budget-of-uncertainty demands (27) (model credited to [7,15,1], i.e., Bertsimas-Sim style); feasibility guaranteed by assuming Σ_i K_i ≥ max{Σ_j d_j : d ∈ D}. Adversarial subproblems were solved via the big-M MIP reformulation with M values set as in Gabrel et al. [13]; CPLEX 12.4, tolerance 1e-4. Illustrative 3×3 instance with a general polyhedral set (g0+g1+g2 ≤ 1.8, g0+g1 ≤ 1.2): C&CG bounds meet at iteration 2 (14 296/35 238 → 33 680/33 680), Benders-dual needs 8 (Table 1). Random instances: 30×30 — average 1.98 s vs 24.19 s CPU (BD/C&CG ratio 16.81), 4.86 vs 49.97 iterations (ratio 11.12); 70×70 — 32.3 s vs 945.82 s (ratio 63.36), 4.93 vs 141.11 iterations (ratio 30.90); master time per iteration nearly equal (ratios 0.95 and 1.01).
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 4 — "For the C&CG algorithm, we first observe that it performs an order of magnitude faster than the Benders-dual algorithm in all experiments."
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 4 — "we assume i Ki ≥ max{ j dj : d ∈ D} to ensure the existence of feasible solutions." (subscripts transcribed from Σ_i, Σ_j)
- our-relevance: Empirical anchor that the speedup comes from fewer iterations, not a faster master — the observation our performance model of cut-and-count schemes must explain.

### fact-zz-6: Few significant scenarios; iteration count insensitive to size
- The authors explain the speedup by C&CG strictly identifying a new significant scenario each iteration (vs Benders-dual spending many iterations reconstructing the value function under one scenario), and by the C&CG master retaining the nominal model's network structure (solvable as a large-scale MIP), unlike accumulated dual cuts. Iteration counts of C&CG are insensitive to problem size, and the number of significant scenarios defining the worst-case cost is relatively stable and small regardless of size — also observed in power system scheduling [21].
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 4 — "We also observe that, unlike computation time, the number of iterations in the C&CG algorithm is insensitive to problem sizes."
- anchor: 12_Zeng_Zhao_2013.pdf, p. 5 — "Those results indicate that the number of significant scenarios defining the worst case cost is relatively stable and small, regardless of the problem size."
- our-relevance: Empirical support for scenario-admission scheduling in our bridge to guaranteed control: a small, stable scenario set is what a staged pipeline with closure moments would converge on.

## Open threads for our portfolio
1. Certified infeasibility: the oracle merely "identifies" an infeasible u* ∈ U; the letter states no certificate (no Farkas-cut statement) for the feasibility side. Our pipelines want the feasibility cut as a verifiable artifact — check the Electronic Companion A2/A3 and later corpus papers (05, 11) for certificate-level statements.
2. Relatively complete recourse is load-bearing for both the O(p) termination and the KKT reformulation (thm-zz-3, thm-zz-4, thm-zz-6); a thread is to state exactness conditions that degrade gracefully without RCR, where this letter only points to problem-specific cuts [18,15].
3. Big-M tightness: convert the "analytically obtained" big-M remark into our gate-artifact pattern — symbolic derivation of M bounds per model class, frozen as path+sha256, used by the judge that validates the adversary MIP.
4. Scenario scheduling: "significant scenarios … stable and small" (fact-zz-6) plus O(p) convergence suggests modeling each scenario admission as a scheduled closure moment with a Prop-2-style stopping certificate — the concrete hook for our guaranteed-control bridge.
5. Master-strength invariant (thm-zz-5) is a candidate cheap mechanical check (MP1 ≤ MP2 on equal scenarios) inside a certified C&CG reimplementation.
6. Provenance/lineage: reconcile the O(pq)/O(p) counting arguments with Chen-Zhang 2009 before adopting this letter's counting as our citation of record for finite convergence of Benders-type schemes in two-stage ARO.
