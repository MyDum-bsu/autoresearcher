## Theorem blocks

### thm-gtw-1: Theorem 1 — value exactness of affine decision rules under (F), (A), (D), (B)
- statement: Consider the two-stage DRO (1a): minimize sup_{P∈P} ρ_P[Q(x;ξ̃)] s.t. x ∈ X, with second-stage (1b) Q(x;ξ) = min { f(x,y;ξ) : A(ξ)x + By ≥ g(ξ), C(ξ)x + Dy ≤ h(ξ), y ∈ R^{n2} }, fixed recourse matrices B ∈ R^{m1×n2}, D ∈ R^{m2×n2}. If there is an x ∈ X optimal in (1) such that:
  - (F) for every ξ ∈ Ξ, f(x,·;ξ) is monotonically non-decreasing in y;
  - (A) the technology matrix A and the right-hand side g are affine functions of ξ;
  - (D) the constraint matrix D is non-negative;
  - (B) there is an index set I ⊆ {1,…,m1}, |I| = n2, such that B_I is invertible with a positive inverse, and [A(ξ)x + By ≥ g(ξ) ⟺ A_I(ξ)x + B_I y ≥ g_I(ξ)] for all ξ ∈ Ξ,
  and problem (1) attains its optimal value, then the optimal values of (1) and of the affine-decision-rule restriction (2) coincide.
- conditions: (B) is required only AT AN OPTIMAL first-stage x and only relative to the whole support Ξ (one fixed index set I, globally over ξ). "Positive inverse" = component-wise non-negative inverse (inverse-positive matrix / M-matrix class). Blanket assumptions: ρ_P is monotonic for every P ∈ P, and ρ_P[Q(x;ξ̃)] is well-defined for all x ∈ X, P ∈ P. Problem (1) must ATTAIN its optimum (value convergence alone is not enough). f may be nonlinear/non-convex; X may be non-convex; no relatively complete recourse assumed; Ξ unrestricted (possibly non-convex). The conclusion is equality of optimal VALUES at an optimal x — not that every ADR-feasible solution is good, and not optimality at suboptimal x. The equivalence in (B) is only claimed for the first (lower-bound) constraint set; (D) makes the second set upper bounds on y.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 8 — `Theorem 1. Assume that problem (1) attains its optimal value and that (F), (A), (D) and (B) are satisfied. Then the optimal values of problems (1) and (2) coincide.` ; (B) stated verbatim on the same page: `(B) There is an index set of constraints I ⊆ {1, . . . , m1}, |I| = n2, such that the restriction B_I of B to those constraints is invertible with a positive inverse, and [A(ξ)x + By ≥ g(ξ) ⟺ A_I(ξ)x + B_I y ≥ g_I(ξ)] ∀ξ ∈ Ξ.` ; (F) and (A) on p. 7, (D) on p. 8.
- our-relevance: This IS our "condition (B)" paper of record — the exact statement to port to our frozen 18-instance family (holds on 10, fails on 8); note it is a four-condition package where (B) alone is not sufficient.

### thm-gtw-2: Pointwise-optimal affine recourse formula (17) — the closed-form "full-information" rule under (B)
- statement: Fix an optimal x ∈ X satisfying (F), (A), (D), (B) with index set I, and let dom Q = {ξ ∈ Ξ : Q(x;ξ) < +∞}. Then (i) every feasible second-stage decision satisfies y(ξ) ≥ B_I^{-1}[g_I(ξ) − A_I(ξ)x] (display (16), a RELAXATION of the first constraint set since B_I^{-1} ≥ 0); (ii) the affine rule y^ℓ(ξ) = B_I^{-1}[g_I(ξ) − A_I(ξ)x] (display (17)) attains (16) with equality, is feasible in the true first constraint set on dom Q, is dominated pointwise by every feasible y(ξ), keeps C(ξ)x + Dy^ℓ(ξ) ≤ h(ξ) satisfied on dom Q via (D), and is pointwise optimal via (F); hence sup_P ρ_P[Q(x;ξ̃)] = sup_P ρ_P[Q(x, y^ℓ(ξ̃);ξ̃)] (display (14)).
- conditions: one fixed I over the whole support; B_I invertible with positive inverse; validity of the feasibility claim of y^ℓ is restricted to dom Q (on Ξ \ dom Q every y is infeasible anyway); the domination y^ℓ(ξ) ≤ y(ξ) uses inverse-positivity, the upper-constraint preservation uses (D), and the optimality uses (F). The paper stresses that (16) is NOT equivalent to the original system — counterexample y1 ≥ 1, y2 ≥ y1 + 2 vs. y1 ≥ 1, y2 ≥ 3 (Figure 7): the feasible regions differ, but both share the component-wise minimal point (1,3), which is all the proof needs.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 42 — `Consider now the solution yℓ(ξ) = B_I^{−1}[gI(ξ) − AI(ξ)x] ∀ξ ∈ Ξ, (17) which satisfies the relaxed constraint set (16) as equality for all ξ ∈ Ξ and which is evidently affine in ξ.` ; p. 43 — `Crucial to our proof of Theorem 1 is the existence of a non-negative inverse B_I^{−1} thanks to assumption (B), which ensures that the constraint system (16) is a relaxation of the first constraint set in (1b) that, if strengthened to equalities as in (17), imposes an affine structure on the second-stage decisions y(ξ).` ; Figure 7 discussion pp. 43–44.
- our-relevance: This is their analogue of our "full-information formula": an explicit affine optimal recourse y = B_I^{-1}(g_I − A_I x), valid exactly because one binding set I is fixed globally and B_I has a positive inverse — a published-style formula of this shape will fail precisely inside a parameter wedge where the binding set rotates, and their theory says so explicitly.

### thm-gtw-3: Proposition 1 — minimality (not necessity) of the four conditions
- statement: The assumptions (F), (A), (D), (B) in Theorem 1 are minimal: the conclusion ceases to hold whenever any one of them is removed (four single-violation counterexamples with uniform ξ ~ U[−1,1], ρ = expectation, no first stage: (F) violated → ADR value 1 vs true 1/2; (A) violated (y ≥ ξ²) → 1 vs 1/3; (D) violated → 1 vs 1/2; (B) violated → 3/2 vs 5/4). Conversely, the conditions are NOT necessary: an instance with Q(ξ) = min{y : y ≥ ξ², y ≥ 2ξ, −y ≤ ξ/2} simultaneously violates (F), (A), (D), (B), yet the optimal second-stage policy y(ξ) = (ξ + 3)/2 is affine.
- conditions: counterexamples use singleton ambiguity sets and expected value (i.e., they bite already for plain stochastic/robust instances); the non-necessity example has no first-stage decisions.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 10 — `Proposition 1. The assumptions (F), (A), (D) and (B) in Theorem 1 are minimal in the sense that the conclusion of the theorem ceases to hold whenever one of the assumptions is removed.` and `We note that despite Proposition 1, the conditions (F), (A), (D) and (B) are not necessary for the optimality of affine decision rules.` ; the (B)-counterexample with the exact gap is on p. 45: `resulting in an objective value of 3/2, whereas for every realization ξ, the second-stage problem Q(ξ) in (1) is optimized by y1(ξ) = min{ξ + 1, 1} and y2(ξ) = max{ξ, 0}, resulting in a lower objective value of 5/4.`
- our-relevance: Two-sided message for our guarantee-gap claim: failure of condition (B) on our 8 instances does NOT by itself certify an ADR gap (non-necessity), but the paper supplies concrete gap witnesses when (B) fails for inverse-degeneracy reasons (3/2 vs 5/4) — templates we can reuse as judges' references.

### thm-gtw-4: Proposition 2 — checkable sufficient condition for (B): dominant constraints + acyclic dependence (partial order / M-matrix)
- statement: Suppose the first constraint set can be written as y_j ≥ α_jk(ξ)^⊤x + β_jk^⊤y + γ_jk(ξ) for j = 1,…,n2, k = 1,…,s_j (display (3)), with β_jk ≥ 0 for all j, k. If at an optimal x ∈ X (i) for every j there is a constraint k_j ∈ {1,…,s_j} that WEAKLY DOMINATES the other s_j − 1 constraints for j under every ξ ∈ Ξ, and (ii) the vectors {β_{j,k_j}}_{j=1}^{n2} form a PARTIAL ORDER (some permutation makes ΠB_IΠ^⊤ strictly upper triangular off-diagonal), then (B) holds at x. Proof: B_I = [e_j^⊤ − β_{j,k_j}^⊤] is a Z-matrix similar to I − (non-negative strictly upper triangular), hence an M-matrix with all eigenvalues 1, hence inverse-positive.
- conditions: needs the β_jk ≥ 0 normalization of system (3) (sign conventions matter: the lower-bound set must see y with non-negative coefficients); (i) alone is necessary-but-not-sufficient for (B) (within systems (3) with β ≥ 0, (B) implies (i)); (ii) alone is neither necessary nor sufficient; jointly sufficient, and minimal in the same sense as Proposition 1. After reordering, the pointwise-optimal recourse is computable by backward substitution: y_1(ξ) = α_{1,k1}(ξ)^⊤x + γ_{1,k1}(ξ), y_2(ξ) = α_{2,k2}(ξ)^⊤x + β_{2,k2,1} y_1 + γ_{2,k2}(ξ), etc.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 11 — `Proposition 2. Assume that the first constraint set A(ξ)x + By ≥ g(ξ) in (1b) can be written as y_j ≥ α_jk(ξ)^⊤x + β_jk^⊤y + γ_jk(ξ) ∀j = 1, . . . , n2, ∀k = 1, . . . , sj, where β_jk ≥ 0 for all j and k.` and `For the constraint system (3) with β_jk ≥ 0 for all j and k, condition (i) in Proposition 2 is by itself necessary but not sufficient for assumption (B), whereas condition (ii) is by itself neither necessary nor sufficient.` ; M-matrix definition on p. 10; proof on p. 46.
- our-relevance: This is the operational test our judges can run per instance to certify (B) without solving anything global: extract per-variable lower-bound systems, check per-ξ weak dominance at the optimal x, then check acyclicity of the β-graph.

### thm-gtw-5: Theorem 2 — K-adaptability restores exactness under piecewise versions (A′), (B′) with K ≥ K_A·K_B
- statement: Replace (A), (B) by: (A′) a covering Ξ = Ξ_1^A ∪ … ∪ Ξ_{K_A}^A with A, g affine on each piece; (B′) a covering Ξ = Ξ_1^B ∪ … ∪ Ξ_{K_B}^B with index sets I_k, |I_k| = n2, each B_{I_k} invertible with a positive inverse and the equivalence [A(ξ)x + By ≥ g(ξ) ⟺ A_{I_k}(ξ)x + B_{I_k}y ≥ g_{I_k}(ξ)] holding on each piece Ξ_k^B. If problem (1) attains its optimal value and (F), (A′), (D), (B′) hold, then the optimal values of (1) and of the K-adaptable affine problem (4) (K candidate affine rules, best chosen after observing ξ) coincide whenever K ≥ K_A · K_B.
- conditions: still pointwise at an optimal x; (F), (D) unchanged; NOTE the warning: (F), (A′), (D), (B′) alone do NOT make a single affine rule optimal (Example 1, see fact-gtw-4); minimal in the sense of Proposition 1; the refined construction uses affine extensions of the piecewise data to the whole support and the monotonicity of ρ_P.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 13 — `Theorem 2. Assume that problem (1) attains its optimal value and that (F), (A′), (D) and (B′) are satisfied. Then the optimal values of problems (1) and (4) coincide whenever K ≥ K_A · K_B.` ; (B′) definition on p. 12.
- our-relevance: If our parameter wedge admits a finite covering on which the binding-set structure is piecewise constant, this theorem converts our wedge counterexamples into a positive statement: finitely many affine rules recover the adaptive optimum.

### thm-gtw-6: Proposition 4 — block-diagonal recourse: parsimonious per-block K-adaptability
- statement: If the second-stage decisions decompose y^⊤ = (y_1^⊤,…,y_P^⊤) with separable objective f = Σ_p f_p(x, y_p; ξ), block-diagonal B = diag(B_1,…,B_P) (A and g only partitioned row-wise, no block diagonality), and the per-block covering condition (B″) holds (each B_p has, on each element of a per-block covering, n_p constraints whose restriction is invertible with positive inverse and equivalent to the block's full first constraint set), then under (F), (A′), (D) the optimal values of (1) and of the decomposed K-adaptability problem (5) coincide whenever K_p ≥ K_A · K_B^p for every block p — i.e., Σ_p K_p rules instead of ∏_p (K_A·K_B^p) full-dimensional rules.
- conditions: separability of f in y across blocks AND block-diagonal B are essential; upper-bound constraints C(ξ)x + Dy ≤ h(ξ) may still couple all blocks (the subvectors participate jointly there); attainment of the optimal value required.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 15 — `Proposition 4. Assume that problem (1) attains its optimal value and that (F), (A′), (D) and (B″) are satisfied. Then the optimal values of problems (1) and (5) coincide whenever Kp ≥ KA·KBp for all p = 1, . . . , P.`
- our-relevance: For our instance family, if a degenerate wedge spoils only a sub-block of the recourse, this quantifies how many affine rules are needed to restore exactness block-wise — useful for a graded exactness profile rather than a binary pass/fail.

### thm-gtw-7: Theorem 3 and Proposition 6 — quantitative suboptimality bounds when exactness fails
- statement: (Theorem 3, supply-chain model with lost sales, which violates Theorem 1's conditions) The relative suboptimality of affine decision rules in the revised supply chain design problem is bounded above by (t̄/t_ + σ/t_) · max_{ξ∈Ξ} [Σ_{g∈G}Σ_{j∈N} l*_{gj}(ξ)] / [Σ_{g∈G}Σ_{j∈N} ξ_gj], where l* is the lost-sales policy of ANY optimal (nonlinear) solution, t̄/t_ are max/min unit transportation costs, σ the lost-sales penalty; in particular ADRs become optimal when the worst-case percentage of lost sales tends to 0. (Proposition 6, unrestricted network) If an optimal solution's distribution network has at most G(N−1)+η arcs, ρ is translation invariant and satisfies the Remark 2 condition, and costs are constant, then the relative suboptimality of any optimal ADR solution is O(ηGC / max_{ξ∈Ξ} ΣΣ ξ_gj) — a distance-to-structured-regime bound.
- conditions: application-specific hypotheses (arborescence-restricted reformulation, rectangular support for Prop 5/6 context, Remark 2 risk condition, translation invariance); bounds are relative to the true optimum and vanish exactly when the instance approaches the regime where (B)-type structure holds.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 23 — `Theorem 3. The relative suboptimality of affine decision rules in the revised supply chain design problem with first stage (6a) and second stage (7) is bounded from above by` (displayed bound as in statement above) ; p. 19 — `the relative suboptimality of any optimal affine decision rule solution is bounded from above by O(ηGC / maxξ∈Ξ Σg∈G Σj∈N ξgj).`
- our-relevance: A published template for turning "condition (B) fails" into a graded guarantee — exactly the shape of our guarantee-gap claim; their bound variable (% of lost sales / number of extra arcs) suggests looking for the analogous degradation parameter in our 8 failing instances.

### thm-gtw-8: Corollary 1 (Appendix A) — conservative K-adaptability with complicating decisions z; condition (B^K)
- statement: For the generalized problem (11) with extra second-stage decisions z ∈ Z(x) (possibly integer / non-convex / x-dependent) whose recourse matrices E(ξ), F(ξ) MAY DEPEND ON ξ, impose (D) and, at an optimal x: (F^K) f(x,·,z;ξ) non-decreasing in y for every (z,ξ); (A^K) A, E and g affine in ξ; (B^K) for EVERY z ∈ Z(x) there is I, |I| = n2, such that B_z is invertible with a positive inverse and [A(ξ)x + E(ξ)z + By ≥ g(ξ) ⟺ A_I(ξ)x + E_I(ξ)z + B_I y ≥ g_I(ξ)] ∀ξ ∈ Ξ. Then the optimal values of the K-adaptability problem (12) (over z) and of its version (13) with affine rules y_k coincide. Remark 4: if |∪_{x∈X} Z(x)| < ∞, (13) recovers the true optimum of (11) for large K. Remark 5: when the weaker set (F^K),(A^K),(D),(B^K) holds but a single affine rule is not optimal, the suboptimality is solely attributable to the z-part.
- conditions: positive-inverse requirement restricted to the coefficient matrix B of the y-part only; z may have random recourse matrices and arbitrary structure; still requires attainment and pointwise (at optimal x) validity.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 38 — `(BK) For every z ∈ Z(x), there is an index set of constraints I ⊆ {1, . . . , m1}, |I| = n2, such that B_z is invertible with a positive inverse, as well as [A(ξ)x + E(ξ)z + By ≥ g(ξ) ⟺ AI(ξ)x + EI(ξ)z + BI y ≥ gI(ξ)] ∀ξ ∈ Ξ.` ; p. 39 — `Corollary 1. Assume that problem (12) attains its optimal value and that (FK), (AK), (D) and (BK) are satisfied. Then the optimal values of problems (12) and (13) coincide.`
- our-relevance: If we model part of our recourse as "complicating", this separates which part of an ADR gap is attributable to the benign y-block (covered by a (B)-type condition) versus the z-block — a clean attribution device for our guarantee-gap bookkeeping.

## Fact blocks

### fact-gtw-1: Problem class and ambiguity classes covered — no support restrictions anywhere
- statement: The paper's scope is fixed-recourse two-stage DRO with possibly nonlinear/non-convex f, non-convex X, uncertain technology matrices A(ξ), C(ξ) and right-hand sides g(ξ), h(ξ), and CONSTANT recourse B, D. The optimality conditions impose NO structure on the support Ξ (which may be non-convex); robust optimization with any uncertainty set Z (as Dirac ambiguity + P-ess sup), stochastic programs, and moment / φ-divergence / Wasserstein DRO are all special cases. Blanket requirement: each ρ_P monotonic (satisfied by coherent measures and VaR; NOT by mean (semi-)moment and mean deviation). Because optimality is POINTWISE (not worst-case), one ADR that is pointwise optimal remains optimal across all these ambiguity classes simultaneously.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 6 — `Problem (1) also encompasses distributionally robust optimization problems with moment and data-driven (e.g., φ-divergence or Wasserstein) ambiguity sets.` ; p. 7 — `For the remainder of the paper, we thus make the blanket assumption that ρ_P is monotonic for every P ∈ P.` ; p. 3 — `again in contrast to the existing optimality results, our conditions ensure point-wise (as opposed to worst-case) optimality of affine decision rules.`
- our-relevance: Our two-stage fixed-recourse setting sits squarely inside (1); the wedge/family questions we study are about (F)–(B), never about the uncertainty geometry — consistent with our claim that no support-side fix can restore exactness.

### fact-gtw-2: Scope caveats — optimal x only, attainment required, no relatively complete recourse needed
- statement: Condition (B) is only required to hold at AN optimal x ∈ X; if problem (1) is feasible but its optimal value is not attained, Theorem 1 makes no statement. Affine rules can be suboptimal at SUBOPTIMAL first-stage decisions even when they are optimal at optimal ones (single-good 3-node network example: unique minimizer f_113(ξ) = min{ξ, 5}, f_123(ξ) = max{ξ−5, 0} is nonlinear at a suboptimal network). No relatively complete recourse is imposed. Remark 2 gives the pointwise-feasibility condition: if sup_P ρ_P[Q(x,y(ξ);ξ);ξ̃] = ∞ whenever Q(x,y(·);ξ) = ∞ for some ξ (true for expectation, CVaR, essential supremum; typically false for VaR), then an ADR is feasible in (2) iff pointwise feasible over Ξ, and the ADR problem admits standard robust reformulations for polyhedral Ξ.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 9 — `Finally, problem (1) may be feasible, but its optimal value may not be attained. In this case, Theorem 1 cannot make any statements as the condition (B) is only required to hold at an optimal first-stage decision x ∈ X.` ; p. 8 — `We emphasize that we do not impose a relatively complete recourse in our results.` ; p. 19 (suboptimal-x example).
- our-relevance: Three audit points for our judges: (a) (B) must be evaluated at an OPTIMAL x of our instances, (b) attainment must be checked before invoking the theorem, (c) our risk-measure choice (expectation/CVaR vs VaR) changes what "ADR feasible" even means.

### fact-gtw-3: A single affine rule genuinely fails under piecewise binding structure (Example 1)
- statement: Example 1: minimize E_P[min{y : y ≥ 1/2 − ξ, y ≥ 0, y ∈ R}] over ξ ~ U[0,1] satisfies (F), (A′), (D) and (B′) (covering Ξ = [0,1/2] ∪ [1/2,1] with I_1 = {1}, I_2 = {2}), yet the unique optimal second-stage policy y*(ξ) = max{1/2 − ξ, 0} is not affine. This is why Theorem 2 needs K ≥ K_A·K_B candidate rules rather than one.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 12 — `Nevertheless, the unique optimal second-stage policy is given by y∗(ξ) = max{1/2 − ξ, 0}, which is evidently not affine.` ; p. 13 — `Unfortunately, the assumptions (F), (A′), (D) and (B′) are not sufficient to guarantee that problem (1) is optimized by an affine decision rule y : Ξ →a Rn2 as the next example shows.`
- our-relevance: Smallest known-to-us published witness that a rotating binding set across a parameter wedge breaks single-ADR exactness while every local piece is affine — likely isomorphic to part of our 8 failing instances; worth an isomorphism check.

### fact-gtw-4: The (B)-violating gap witness — degenerate (non-inverse-positive) B_I
- statement: The (B)-part of Proposition 1: Q(ξ) = min{y_1 + 2y_2 : y_1 + y_2 ≥ ξ + 1, y_1, y_2 ≥ 0, y_1, y_2 ≤ 1} on ξ ~ U[−1,1]. For every ξ > −1 the constraint y_1 + y_2 ≥ ξ + 1 must belong to I, but every candidate 2×2 restriction B_I ∈ {(1 1; 1 0), (1 1; 0 1)} lacks a positive inverse, so (B) fails; the ADR optimum is y_1* = y_2* = (ξ+1)/2 with value 3/2, while the true pointwise optimum is y_1 = min{ξ+1, 1}, y_2 = max{ξ, 0} with value 5/4. The failure mode is inverse-positivity (M-matrix) degeneracy, not non-affinity of the optimum.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 45 — `none of the coefficient matrices B_I ∈ {(1 1; 1 0), (1 1; 0 1)} have a positive inverse, that is, assumption (B) is violated.` ; p. 10 (M-matrix definition) — `a Z-matrix is called an M-matrix if all of its eigenvalues have a non-negative real part. The M-matrices form an important subclass of the inverse positive matrices, that is, the matrices that possess a component-wise non-negative inverse.`
- our-relevance: The minimal published instance where (B) fails BECAUSE the recourse matrix is inverse-degenerate, with an exact gap (3/2 vs 5/4) — a candidate frozen reference instance for our guarantee-gap judge, and a diagnostic to distinguish from fact-gtw-3's wedge failure mode.

### fact-gtw-5: Formulation-sensitivity warning — an epigraph reformulation can destroy ADR optimality
- statement: The instance minimize E[Q̃(ξ)] with Q̃(ξ) = min{max{y, 0} : y ≥ ξ, y ∈ R} (ξ ~ U[−1,1]) satisfies (F), (A), (D), (B) and attains optimal value 1/4 with the affine rule y*(ξ) = ξ. Replacing the objective by its epigraph form Q̂(ξ) = min{τ : τ ≥ max{y,0}, y ≥ ξ} and restricting BOTH τ(ξ) and y(ξ) to be affine yields the strictly larger value 1/2. Hence Theorem 1 would be less general under affine f plus epigraphical reformulations; nonlinearity of f in y (allowed by (F)) is load-bearing. The authors similarly warn that piecewise-affine cost functions p_ec cannot be epigraph-linearized without sacrificing ADR optimality.
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 29 — `This instance evidently satisfies the assumptions (F), (A), (D) and (B) of Theorem 1 and its optimal value 1/4 is attained by the affine decision rule y∗(ξ) = ξ.` and, after the displayed reformulation, `to affine decision rules τ(ξ) and y(ξ), however, attains the strictly larger objective value of 1/2.`
- our-relevance: Directly supports our methodology: a "published-style full-information formula" can fail because of the algebraic formulation of the instance rather than genuine non-exactness — our 18-instance family must fix canonical formulations before any formula-failure verdict.

### fact-gtw-6: Generalizations and solution machinery around Theorem 1
- statement: Remark 1: if A and g belong to a function class C closed under linear combinations (piecewise affine, polynomial, trigonometric), Theorem 1 holds verbatim with decision rules from C (nonlinearities absorbable into the support via liftings). Remark 2 catalogs ADR solution methods (monolithic reformulations via scenario fans/duality, Benders and semi-infinite schemes) — the C&CG-style schemes of Zeng-Zhao 2013 appear only in this background role. Observation 1 + Proposition 5: supply-chain design with arborescence networks (and, under proportional costs t_gij = τ_g·b_ij with rectangular support, even the unrestricted version) is optimally solved in ADRs; Observation 5: flexible production planning with acyclic bills of materials likewise (via Proposition 2: big-M-selected weakly dominant constraints + topological order).
- anchor: 24_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2025.pdf, p. 9 — `If the technology matrix A and the right-hand side vector g belong to a function class C that is closed under linear combinations (e.g., piecewise affine, polynomial or trigonometric functions), then Theorem 1 continues to hold if we replace the class of affine decision rules in problem (2) with the broader class of decision rules in C.` ; p. 2 — `semi-infinite programming (Zeng and Zhao 2013, Ayoub and Poss 2016)` (background listing of solution schemes) ; p. 18 — `Proposition 5. Assume that (i) all distributions P ∈ P share the same rectangular support Ξ; that (ii) the ambiguous risk measure ρ satisfies the condition of Remark 2; and that (iii) there is τ ∈ R+G such that t_gij = τg · b_ij for all g ∈ G and i, j ∈ N. Then the unrestricted version of problem (6) is optimally solved in affine decision rules.`

## Landscape
- Position within ADR-optimality literature (their own survey, pp. 4–5): worst-case optimality was previously known only for special uncertainty geometries coupled to problem structure — simplex uncertainty (Bertsimas-Goyal 2012), hyperrectangle demands in multi-product inventory (Bertsimas et al. 2010), supermodular objectives with corner-polytope supports (Iancu et al. 2013), intersections of 1-/∞-norm balls (Ardestani-Jaafari & Delage 2016), budgeted/budget-type sets with tree networks (El Housni-Goyal 2021; Simchi-Levi et al. 2019), and the geometry-independent exception of robust vehicle routing (Gounaris et al. 2013). GTW-2025's claim to novelty: (i) minimal conditions (Proposition 1) with NO support restrictions, so exactness is a property of the constraint/recourse algebra rather than of the uncertainty geometry; (ii) POINTWISE rather than worst-case optimality, which transfers to Wasserstein/φ-divergence DRO (they state no prior ADR-optimality results existed for DRO); (iii) extension to K-adaptability and to block-decomposed recourse.
- Relation to our anchors: Ben-Tal et al. 2004 (AARC) is cited (p. 2) only as the historical resurfacing of ADRs; Bertsimas-de Ruiter 2016 (dual AARC bounds) appears only in Remark 3's bibliographical notes (p. 26) as applications of SUBOPTIMAL ADRs (lot sizing, facility location on bipartite graphs); Zeng-Zhao 2013 C&CG appears (p. 2, p. 9) as one of several solution schemes for the ADR problem (2), not as an exactness result. GTW-2025 therefore occupies the exactness layer that is complementary to the C&CG/dual-bound layers our portfolio already covers.
- Proof technology: positive-inverse (M-matrix) linear algebra + pointwise domination; the K-adaptability and block results are covering-refinement arguments; no duality is used anywhere — in contrast to the dual/lifting techniques in the AARC tradition.
