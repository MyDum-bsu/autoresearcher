Notation normalization used below (mirroring the source's own macros): two-stage
problem `(RLP)`: `min_{x∈X, y(·)} c^T x + max_{u∈U} d^T y(u)` s.t.
`Ax + B y(u) ≥ F u ∀ u∈U`; note the source macro `\AR` literally prints "RLP"
(`arlp.tex:21`) and `\AP` prints "Aff" (`arlp.tex:22`). Uncertainty set
homogenized: `U = {u ∈ K : u_1 = 1}` with `K ⊆ R_+ × R^{k-1}` a closed convex
full-dimensional cone; `Q = K × R^m_+` (`arlp.tex:51`). Dual feasible set
`W = {w ≥ 0 : B^T w = d}`; `z = (u,w) ∈ R^{k+m}`;
`E = (−d e_1^T, B^T) ∈ R^{n_2 × (k+m)}`; `g_1 = (e_1, 0)`;
`G(x) = [[0, (F−Axe_1^T)^T], [F−Axe_1^T, 0]]`;
`H(Y) = [[−e_1 d^T Y − Y^T d e_1^T, (BY)^T], [BY, 0]]`. Inner approximation
cone `IA(Q)` (macro `\M` prints "IA"): symmetric `S` with
`S_{11} = e_1 α^T + α e_1^T, α ∈ K^*`, `Rows(S_{21}) ∈ K^*`, `S_{22} ≥ 0`.

## Theorem blocks

### thm-xb-1: Strong duality for the copositive dual of the adversary problem
- statement: Under relatively complete recourse (Assumption 4), let `r > 0` be a
  constant such that `z^T z ≤ r` holds for all `u ∈ U` and all extreme points
  `w ∈ W` (`z = (u,w)`). Then the optimal value of the copositive program
  `min { λ + rρ : λ g_1g_1^T − ½G(x) + ½(E^TΛ^T + ΛE) + ρI ∈ COP(Q), ρ ≥ 0 }`
  equals `π(x)`, the worst-case recourse cost at first-stage `x`.
- conditions: Assumption 4 (relatively complete recourse) — used both to bound
  the extreme points of `W` (radius polynomially computable by
  Schrijver 1986, `arlp.tex:426-430`) and to make Slater's condition hold (large
  `ρ` makes the matrix positive definite, `arlp.tex:471-474`).
- anchor: arlp.tex:477-480 — `Under Assumption \ref{ass:rcr}, suppose $r > 0$ is a constant such that` / `$z^T z \le r$ is satisfied by all $u \in \U$ and all extreme points $w$ / `\in \W$, where $z = (u,w)$. Then the optimal value of (\ref{equ:cop_qx}) equals $\p(x)$.`
- our-relevance: the exactness certificate for the infinite-dimensional adversary
  is a single copositivity inequality plus Slater — the conic analogue of the
  LP-certificate/condition-(B) optimality gates our judges encode; strong duality
  here is what turns "affine in x" into a single-level exact reformulation.

### thm-xb-2: Exact copositive reformulation of the two-stage problem (main theorem)
- statement: The optimal value of `(RLP̄)` —
  `min_{x,λ,Λ,ρ} c^T x + λ + rρ` s.t. `x ∈ X`,
  `λ g_1g_1^T − ½G(x) + ½(E^TΛ^T + ΛE) + ρI ∈ COP(Q)`, `ρ ≥ 0` —
  equals `v*_{RLP}`, the true two-stage adjustable robust value. Hence linear
  optimization over an explicitly given copositive cone solves the ARO problem
  exactly.
- conditions: Assumptions 1-4 (tractable `X` and `K`, feasibility, boundedness,
  relatively complete recourse); compact convex `U` homogenized as a slice of
  `K`; rational data for the radius `r` argument.
- anchor: arlp.tex:496-497 — `\begin{theorem} \label{thm:equivalence}` / `The optimal value of ($\overline{RLP}$) equals $v^*_{\AR}$.`
- our-relevance: this is the paper-side answer to "when do simple policies
  suffice" pushed to the limit — the exact policy class here is implicitly
  "copositive policies", provably rich enough for full adaptability; it frames
  affine-rule exactness as the question of when the copositive optimum is
  attained already at the `IA(Q)` slice (see thm-xb-4/thm-xb-6).

### thm-xb-3: Exact copositive reformulation of the affine policy
- statement: `(Aff̄)`: `min_{x,λ,Y} c^T x + λ` s.t. `x ∈ X`,
  `λ g_1g_1^T − ½G(x) + ½H(Y) ∈ COP(Q)` has optimal value `v*_{Aff}`, the
  affine-policy value. (Derived by dualizing the inner min over `y` with the
  constraint `y(u) = Yu` kept only in the objective, homogenizing, and applying
  copositive duality; Slater holds because `K` has interior.)
- conditions: same Assumptions 1-3; no recourse assumption needed beyond
  feasibility of the affine policy; strong duality for the per-`x` copositive
  program is unconditional here.
- anchor: arlp.tex:614-615 — `\begin{proposition} %\label{thm:equivalence2}` / `    The optimal value of (\ref{equ:cop_ap}) is $v^*_{\AP}$. `
- our-relevance: expresses the affine-policy value itself as a copositive
  program, so the gap `v*_{RLP} ≤ v*_{Aff}` becomes a pure cone-inclusion
  statement (`IA(Q) ⊆ COP(Q)` applied to different matrix structures) — exactly
  the reformulation our exactness-of-ADR line needs to compare policy classes
  structurally rather than by examples.

### thm-xb-4: The tractable IA cone reproduces the affine policy exactly
- statement: The `IA(Q)` problem `(IA)`: `min_{x,λ,Y} c^T x + λ` s.t.
  `λ g_1g_1^T − ½G(x) + ½H(Y) ∈ IA(Q)` satisfies `v*_{IA} = v*_{Aff}`; by
  construction `IA(Q) ⊆ COP(Q)` (Lemma, `arlp.tex:640-641`), yet replacing
  `COP(Q)` by the tractable cone `IA(Q)` loses nothing: every feasible
  `(x, Y, λ)` of the standard conic affine reformulation maps to a feasible `S`
  of `(IA)` with equal objective (explicit block decomposition with
  `α = ½(λe_1 − Y^T d)`).
- conditions: none beyond the standing assumptions; the proof is constructive
  and uses only `λe_1 − Y^Td ∈ K^*`, `Rows(Axe_1^T − F + BY) ∈ K^*` of the
  conic affine program.
- anchor: arlp.tex:682-683 — `\begin{theorem} \label{theorem:affine-tractable}` / `$v_{\M}^* = v_{\AP}^*$.`
- our-relevance: the key structural identification — "affine policy" =
  "copositive reformulation restricted to the tractable inner cone `IA`". In our
  terms: the affine rule is exact iff the copositive optimum admits an
  `IA(Q)`-representation, i.e. exactness becomes membership of an optimal
  dual matrix in an explicit polyhedral-in-`K^*` cone — an LP/SDP-checkable
  certificate shape.

### thm-xb-5: IA is a structured restriction of the exact copositive program
- statement: Writing the dual multiplier `Λ = (Λ_1; Λ_2)` with
  `Λ_1 ∈ R^{k×n_2}`, `Λ_2 ∈ R^{m×n_2}`, problem `(IA)` is exactly the
  restriction of `(RLP̄)` obtained by setting `Λ_2 = 0`, `ρ = 0`, identifying
  `Y = Λ_1^T`, and tightening `COP(Q)` to `IA(Q)` (under which
  `E^TΛ^T + ΛE = H(Y)`).
- conditions: none; purely algebraic identification of the two matrix forms.
- anchor: arlp.tex:731-733 — `\in \RR^{m \times n_2}$. Problem $(\M)$ is a restriction of` / `(\ref{equ:cop_ar}) in which $\Lambda_2 = 0$, $Y$ is identified with` / `$\Lambda_1^T$, $\rho = 0$, and $\COP(\Q)$ is tightened to $\M(\Q)$.`
- our-relevance: gives the precise coordinates in which "more adaptive than
  affine" = "free `Λ_2`, free `ρ`, bigger cone"; for our exactness conditions
  this localizes where affine suboptimality can come from (the `Λ_2`-block,
  i.e. dual variables interacting the recourse constraints against `w`), a
  concrete object our condition-(B) analysis could characterize.

### thm-xb-6: The sandwich theorem (any in-between cone improves the affine policy)
- statement: For any closed convex cone `IB(Q)` with
  `IA(Q) ⊆ IB(Q) ⊆ COP(Q)`, the problem `(IB)`: `min_{x,λ,Λ} c^T x + λ` s.t.
  `λ g_1g_1^T − ½G(x) + ½(E^TΛ^T + ΛE) ∈ IB(Q)` satisfies
  `v*_{RLP} ≤ v*_{IB} ≤ v*_{Aff}`.
- conditions: standing assumptions for the left inequality (via thm-xb-2); the
  right inequality holds even without relatively complete recourse (the
  `ρ = 0` / no-radius version remains a valid restriction of a valid upper
  bound, cf. fact-xb-5).
- anchor: arlp.tex:783-784 — `\begin{theorem} \label{theorem:approximaterlp}` / `$v_{\AR}^* \le v_{\IB}^* \le v_{\AP}^*$.`
- our-relevance: the paper's headline guarantee and a template for a whole
  family of "at least as strong as ADR" approximations parameterized by cone
  choice; our exactness question becomes the complementary question of when the
  left inequality is tight at the `IB = IA` end (always, by thm-xb-4) versus at
  intermediate cones.

### thm-xb-7: Generic construction of in-between cones (hierarchy interface)
- statement: If `IB(K) ⊆ COP(K)` is any inner approximation of the copositive
  cone of the uncertainty cone `K` itself, then the set
  `IB(Q) = { S + M + R : S ∈ IA(Q), M ⪰ 0, R_{11} ∈ IB(K), R_{21} = R_{22} = 0 }`
  satisfies `IA(Q) ⊆ IB(Q) ⊆ COP(Q)`, so every inner approximation of `COP(K)`
  lifts to a certified affine-policy improver for `(RLP)`.
- conditions: `IB(K) ⊆ COP(K)` closed convex cone; proof is a three-term
  nonnegativity argument on `p ∈ K`, `q ≥ 0`.
- anchor: arlp.tex:794-806 — `Suppose $\IB(\K) \subseteq \COP(\K)$, and define` ... `Then $\M(\Q) \subseteq \IB(\Q) \subseteq \COP(\Q)$.` (conclusion at arlp.tex:806)
- our-relevance: modularity result — any progress on approximating `COP(K)`
  (RLT, SDP hierarchies) converts automatically into a policy-class improvement
  with the sandwich guarantee; for our portfolio it separates "policy class"
  questions from "cone relaxation" questions, which is the same decoupling our
  judges rely on (generation vs. verdict).

### thm-xb-8: Provable exactness class — temporal networks where SDP closes an unbounded affine gap
- statement: For the temporal-network problem of Wiesemann-Kuhn-Rustem 2011
  with ball-type uncertainty (`Ξ_2`, norm-2 cone `K_2`): the true value is
  `v*_{RLP,2} = ½(√s + s)` (Lemma `lemma:calculateVar2`, `arlp.tex:893-895`)
  while the affine value for both 1-norm and 2-norm sets is `v*_{Aff} = s`, an
  arbitrarily large gap as `s → ∞`. For `(IB_2)` with the SOC instantiation
  `COP(K_2) = {τJ + M_{11} : τ ≥ 0, M_{11} ⪰ 0}`, for every `ρ > 0` the
  program has a feasible solution of value `v*_{RLP,2} + rρ`
  (`arlp.tex:1035-1039`), hence letting `ρ → 0`: `v*_{IB,2} = v*_{RLP,2}` —
  the semidefinite approximation is exact, in polynomial time. For the 1-norm
  set (`K_1` polyhedral, RLT-type cone), the same value `½(√s + s)` persists:
  `v*_{IB,1} = ½(√s + s)`, strictly between `v*_{RLP,1} = ½(√s+1)` and
  `v*_{Aff,1} = s` (`arlp.tex:1113-1115`).
- conditions: the specific serial temporal-network structure (`x = 0`,
  `B` incidence-like with paired rows, `d = e_s`); `K_2` second-order cone
  (self-dual, exact copositive description by Sturm-Zhang 2003); feasibility
  argument uses the auxiliary PSD-lift lemma below (fact on
  `ρI + V + μE^TE ≻ 0`).
- anchor: arlp.tex:1045-1046 — `\begin{theorem}` / `$v_{\IB,2}^* = v_{\AR,2}^*$` (feasibility step: arlp.tex:1036-1037 — `For any $\rho > 0$, (\ref{equ:temporal1})` / `has a feasible solution with objective value $v_{\AR,2}^* + r` / `\rho$.`; strict-improvement claim: arlp.tex:1113-1114 — `$v_{\IB,1}^* = v_{\IB,2}^* = \tfrac12(\sqrt{s} + s)$.`)
- our-relevance: the first fully worked "affine fails arbitrarily badly, SDP
  solves exactly" family — the canonical counterexample pool and stress test for
  any exactness condition for affine rules we formulate: our conditions must
  fail on this class, and ideally predict for which cones (`K_1` vs `K_2`)
  exactness holds.

## Fact blocks

### fact-xb-1: Problem class and homogenized uncertainty set
- statement: The paper covers two-stage adjustable robust LP with uncertain
  right-hand sides: `min_{x,y(·)} c^T x + max_{u∈U} d^T y(u)` s.t.
  `Ax + B y(u) ≥ F u ∀ u ∈ U`, `x ∈ X` closed convex; `U ⊆ R^k` compact convex
  nonempty, modeled as `U = {u ∈ K : u_1 = 1}` for a closed convex
  full-dimensional cone `K ⊆ R_+ × R^{k-1}` (homogenization also captures
  affine dependence of uncertain parameters).
- anchor: arlp.tex:154-156 — `v_{\AR}^* := & \min \limits_{x,y(\cdot)} & c^Tx + \max \limits_{u \in \U} d^Ty(u) \\` / `         & \st & Ax + By(u) \ge Fu \ \ \ \forall \; u \in \U \\` / `&     & x \in \X,` ; arlp.tex:169 — `\U := \{u \in \K : e_1^Tu = u_1 = 1\},`
- applies: general convex-cone-representable uncertainty (polyhedral, SOC, SD);
  no integer first stage; objective has no `u`-dependent cost matrix (`F` enters
  constraints only) — narrower than BGT-2004's general form, precisely to make
  the dual-side homogenization clean.

### fact-xb-2: Assumption profile, including relatively complete recourse
- statement: Four standing assumptions: (1) `X` and `K` computationally
  tractable (`K` full-dimensional); (2) `(RLP)` feasible; (3) `v*_{RLP}` finite;
  (4) relatively complete recourse: the innermost LP `min{d^Ty : By ≥ Fu − Ax}`
  is feasible for all `x ∈ X`, `u ∈ U`. Under (2)+(3) the dual set
  `W = {w ≥ 0 : B^T w = d}` is nonempty; (4) additionally makes every dual
  optimal at an extreme point of `W` (which lie in a ball `w^Tw ≤ r_w`,
  `r_w` polynomially computable from `B, d` — Schrijver 1986, `arlp.tex:426-430`).
- anchor: arlp.tex:230-231 — `Problem $(\AR)$ possesses relatively complete recourse, i.e., for all $x` / `\in \X$ and $u \in \U$, the innermost LP in (\ref{equ:ar'}) is feasible.` ; arlp.tex:225-226 — `\W := \{ w \ge 0 : B^T w = d \}`
- applies: the whole exactness chain (thm-xb-1, thm-xb-2); this is the same
  relatively-complete-recourse tier that Bertsimas-Shtern 2018 call Assumption A
  — here it is what makes the copositive dual exact rather than merely bounding.

### fact-xb-3: The adversary is a bilinear program over `U × W` and is NP-hard
- statement: By LP strong duality, `π(x) = max_{u∈U} min_y {d^Ty : By ≥ Fu−Ax}
  = max_{(u,w) ∈ U×W} (Fu − Ax)^T w` — a bilinear program over convex sets,
  "NP-hard in general" (citing Konno 1976).
- anchor: arlp.tex:379-380 — `\p(x) = \max_{u \in \U} \max_w \{ (Fu - Ax)^T w : w \in \W \}` / `          = \max_{(u,w) \in \U \times \W} \ (Fu - Ax)^T w,` ; arlp.tex:383-384 — `In words, $\p(x)$ equals the optimal value of a bilinear program over` / `convex constraints, which is NP-hard in general \cite{Konno.1976}.`
- applies: any two-stage problem with RHS uncertainty; this is the same dual
  wedge `W` that appears in Bertsimas-de Ruiter 2016 and Bertsimas-Shtern 2018 —
  the bilinear `(u,w)` objective is exactly what their alternating-maximization
  heuristics attack, and what the copositive lift here solves exactly.

### fact-xb-4: Copositive representation of the adversary (lift-and-linearize)
- statement: Define `z = (u,w)`, `E = (−d e_1^T, B^T)`. Homogenizing the
  bilinear program gives `π(x) = max {(F − Axe_1^T) • Z_{21} : ZE^T = 0,
  Z ∈ CP(Q), g_1g_1^T • Z = 1}` (the radius-relaxed version adds
  `I • Z ≤ r` without changing the value, `arlp.tex:440-444`). Source-level
  caveat: the displayed block structure of `Z` at `arlp.tex:409-412` prints
  `Z_{21}` in the bottom-right block where `Z_{22}` is meant (apparent typo in
  this TeX version; the dual problems and proofs use `Z_{22}`).
- anchor: arlp.tex:420-422 — `\p(x) = \max \; & (F - Axe_1^T) \bullet Z_{21} \\` / `\st \; \; & ZE^T = 0 \nonumber \\` / `     & Z \in \CP(\Q), \quad g_1 g_1^T \bullet Z = 1. \nonumber`
- applies: any bilinear max over `U × W` with `W` in equality form; the recipe
  (homogenize by `u_1 = 1`, lift `zz^T`, impose `EZ = 0` via completely
  positive cone, linearize) is reusable verbatim for our wedge-W full-information
  formulations.

### fact-xb-5: Upper-bound validity without relatively complete recourse
- statement: Even if Assumption 4 fails and strong duality between the lifted
  primal and the copositive dual cannot be established, the optimal value of
  `(RLP̄)` (in the variant without the `I•Z ≤ r` radius constraint, `ρ = 0`) is
  still a valid upper bound on `v*_{RLP}` and remains at least as strong as the
  affine policy; both numerical non-RCR examples rely on this.
- anchor: arlp.tex:514-518 — `We remark that, even if Assumption \ref{ass:rcr} fails and strong` / `duality between (\ref{equ:qxcpp'}) and (\ref{equ:cop_qx}) cannot be` / `established, it still holds that the optimal value of ($\overline{RLP}$)` / `is an upper bound on $v^*_{\AR}$.`
- applies: practical instances where RCR is unknown or false (their newsvendor:
  "We do not know if Assumption 4 necessarily holds", `arlp.tex:1306-1307`);
  mirrors the condition-(B) fallback in our portfolio: weaker recourse profile
  degrades exactness to one-sided validity, not the method.

### fact-xb-6: Tractable conic reformulation of the affine policy
- statement: The affine policy `(Aff)` (with free `Y ∈ R^{n_2×k}`,
  `y(u) = Yu`) is equivalent, by standard BGT-2004 robust-reformulation
  techniques, to the conic program `min_{x,Y,λ} c^T x + λ` s.t.
  `λ e_1 − Y^T d ∈ K^*`, `Rows(Axe_1^T − F + BY) ∈ K^*`, `x ∈ X` — tractable
  whenever `K^*` is; in general `v*_{RLP} < v*_{Aff}`.
- anchor: arlp.tex:543-545 — `\min \limits_{x,Y,\lambda}  & c^Tx + \lambda \\` / ` \st & \lambda e_1 - Y^Td \in {\K}^*   \\` / `      & \text{Rows}(Axe_1^T - F + BY) \in {\K}^* \\`
- applies: the affine-policy baseline for all cone types in the paper; the
  exact input to thm-xb-4's identification `v*_{IA} = v*_{Aff}`.

### fact-xb-7: Explicit copositive cones for SOC and polyhedral uncertainty
- statement: For the second-order cone `K = {‖(u_2..u_k)‖ ≤ u_1}`,
  `COP(K) = {R_{11} = τJ + M_{11} : τ ≥ 0, M_{11} ⪰ 0}` with
  `J = Diag(1,−1,…,−1)` (Sturm-Zhang 2003) — an exact SDP representation, so
  one may take `IB(K) = COP(K)` in practice. For a polyhedral cone
  `K = {Pu ≥ 0}`, a standard inner approximation is
  `IB(K) = {R_{11} = P^T N P : N ≥ 0}` (RLT; Anstreicher 2009, Burer 2015,
  Sherali-Adams 2013). Complete hierarchies exist (Zuluaga et al. 2006).
- anchor: arlp.tex:827-829 — `it is known \cite{Sturm.Zhang.2003} that` / `\[` / `   \COP(\K) = \{ R_{11} = \tau J + M_{11} : \tau \ge 0, \ M_{11} \succeq 0 \},` ; arlp.tex:838 — `\IB(\K) := \{ R_{11} = P^T N P : N \ge 0 \},`
- applies: instantiation of thm-xb-7 for the two cone families used in all
  examples; explains why SOC instances come out exact (no relaxation gap in the
  cone) while the polyhedral instance does not fully close (thm-xb-8, case `j=1`).

### fact-xb-8: Numerical evidence — gap closure and running times
- statement: (i) Multi-item newsvendor (polyhedral `K`, non-RCR instance from
  Ardestani-Jaafari-Delage 2016): `v*_{RLP} = −825.83`, `v*_{Aff} = −41.83`,
  `v*_{IB} = −411.08` — about half the affine-vs-LB gap closed
  (`arlp.tex:1169-1177`). (ii) Network lot-sizing (`N = 8`, ball uncertainty,
  SOC): LB 1573.8, `v*_{Aff} = 1950.8`, `v*_{IB} = 1794.0`; with a budget
  uncertainty set instead, the method "did not perform better than the affine
  policy" (`arlp.tex:1273-1274`). (iii) 1000 random instances
  (`(k,m,n_1,n_2) = (17,16,3,5)`, SOC): 971 had `v*_{IB} = v*_{Aff}`; 29 with a
  positive gap closed on average 20.2% of the affine-vs-LB gap
  (`arlp.tex:1314-1318`). Times: 0.23-35 s on Mosek 8 (Table, `arlp.tex:1344-1346`).
- anchor: arlp.tex:1315-1318 — `all 1,000 instances, 971 have $v^*_{\text{LB}} < v_{\IB}^* = v_{\AP}^*$,` / `while the remaining 29 have $v^*_{\text{LB}} < v_{\IB}^* < v_{\AP}^*$.` / `For those 29 instances with a positive gap, the average relative gap` / `closed is 20.2\%, where` ; arlp.tex:1170-1172 — `in \cite{Gorissen.Hertog.2013}, we have $v_{\AR}^* = -825.83$ (so` / `Assumption \ref{ass:boundedval} holds). Moreover, the affine-policy` / `value is $v_{\AP}^*=-41.83$, and thus Assumption \ref{ass:feas} holds.`
- applies: SOC-based instances of the given sizes; the budget-set non-result and
  the 971/1000 exactness rate are both relevant priors for when ADR/improved
  cones matter empirically.

  copositive duality to a two-stage ARO adversary, exploiting RCR for strong
  duality rather than approximating directly. Chen-Zhang 2009 /
  Bertsimas-Iancu-Parrilo 2011 — orthogonal policy-enrichment line: here
  adaptability is enriched on the cone side (hierarchy `IA ⊆ IB ⊆ COP`) instead
  of on the functional side (extended affine / polynomial policies).
- contradicts-or-refines: refines the folk claim "affine policy is the
  tractable approximation" by exhibiting a polynomial-time-solvable instance
  family with arbitrarily large affine gap (thm-xb-8) — i.e. tractability of
  `(RLP)` itself for non-polyhedral `U`; simultaneously tempers it: for budget
  (polyhedral) uncertainty the same machinery gave no improvement
  (`arlp.tex:1273-1274`), and for `K_1` the RLT cone stalls strictly above the
  robust value (`arlp.tex:1113-1114`). Explicitly flags redundancy with
  Hanasusanto-Kuhn 2016 (equivalent exactness result, `arlp.tex:301-303`),
  a simultaneous-discovery event any exactness-line citation graph must record.

## Open threads for our portfolio

- The paper's own open question is precisely our ADR-exactness line inverted:
  "whether the copositive approach corresponds to enforcing a particular class
  of policies `y(·)`" and "how to extract a specific policy `y(·)` from the
  solution of the approximation `(IB)`" (anchor: arlp.tex:1361-1367). The
  copositive reformulation is exact but policy-opaque — if "copositive policies"
  were characterized, affine exactness would become the question of when the
  copositive optimum collapses to the `IA(Q)` face (thm-xb-5's `Λ_2 = 0, ρ = 0`
  slice). A concrete formalization target for conj-ldr-exactness.
- Exactness class certificate: thm-xb-8 shows the SOC cone is exact on temporal
  networks because `COP(K_2)` itself is SDP-representable (fact-xb-7), while
  the polyhedral `K_1` case leaves a strict gap. Open: an a priori, structural
  condition on `(A,B,F,K)` (not cone-representation-dependent) predicting when
  `v*_{IB} = v*_{RLP}` — our exactness-condition machinery could aim to
  reproduce `½(√s+s)` on this family from first principles.
- Dual wedge reuse: `π(x) = max_{(u,w) ∈ U×W} (Fu−Ax)^T w` (fact-xb-3) is the
  same bilinear/wedge structure as our published full-information formula and
  Bertsimas-Shtern's DDBD detector; the copositive lift (fact-xb-4) is the
  exact-global side of that same object — worth one comparison block: when does
  our LP-certificate machinery certify the same value the SDP lift proves?
- The RCR-optional upper bound (fact-xb-5) plus the strict two-tier assumption
  profile (fact-xb-2) is a second published instance (after Bertsimas-Shtern
  2018) of condition-(A)-for-exactness vs condition-(B)-for-validity — supports
  making RCR-vs-feasibility an explicit axis in our exactness taxonomy.
- Empirical priors for our solvers: 971/1000 random SOC instances had
  `v*_{IB} = v*_{Aff}` (fact-xb-8) — i.e. the affine rule was exactly optimal
  in the value even when no policy is extracted; this is published, citable
  evidence for ADR near-exactness frequency, adjacent to our
  ADR-often-optimal observations.
- Minor source hygiene: block-structure typo `Z_{21}` for `Z_{22}` at
  `arlp.tex:409-412`; macro `\AR` renders as "RLP" so `(RLP)`/`(RLP̄)` in the
  PDF mean the ARO problem and its copositive reformulation, not an LP
  relaxation.
