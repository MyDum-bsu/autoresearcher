## Theorem blocks

### thm-bgt-1: ARC = RC under constraint-wise uncertainty and compact recourse
- statement: If the uncertain LP satisfies (1) constraint-wise uncertainty — there is a partition ξ = (ξ¹, ..., ξᵐ) into non-overlapping sub-vectors such that for every i and all u, v the i-th residual (U(ξ)u + V(ξ)v − b(ξ))ᵢ depends on ξⁱ only, and Z = Z₁ × ... × Zₘ with each Zᵢ nonempty convex compact; and (2) recourse boundedness — for every u feasible for the ARC there is a compact set V_u such that every v with U(ζ)u + V(ζ)v ≤ b(ζ) for some ζ ∈ Z lies in V_u — then the RC and the ARC are equivalent (same feasible u's and same optimal value).
- conditions: normalized uncertain LP (objective independent of adjustable part); both assumptions jointly required; the authors themselves call these conditions "quite stringent".
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 5 (printed 354–355) — `Then the RC of LP_𝒵 is equivalent to its ARC.` (conditions 1–2 quoted on the same pages, starting `Theorem 2.1. Let LP_𝒵 (3) satisfy the following two assumptions:`)
- our-relevance: This is the paper's only general ARC-exactness statement; its two structural conditions (constraint-wise uncertainty, compactness of the recourse set per u) are candidate ingredients for our exactness-condition profile (conj-ldr-exactness boundary cases).

### thm-bgt-2: Finite-uncertainty ARC = LP with one recourse block per vertex
- statement (Theorem 2.2, attributed to [12] = Goryashko's Master's thesis): Assume the uncertainty set is the convex hull of finitely many data realizations, Z = Conv{[U₁, V₁, b₁], ..., [U_N, V_N, b_N]} (display (A)). Then, in the fixed-recourse case V₁ = ... = V_N, the ARC is given by the usual LP min over u, v₁, ..., v_N of cᵀu s.t. U_ℓu + Vv_ℓ ≤ b_ℓ for ℓ = 1, ..., N — i.e., one recourse block v_ℓ per vertex of the uncertainty set — and is therefore computationally tractable.
- conditions: fixed recourse (all V_ℓ equal); Z given as the convex hull of a finite list (vertices); normalized objective (no v in cᵀu).
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 5 (printed 355) — `Then in the case V_1 = ... = V_N of fixed recourse the ARC is given by the usual LP min_{u,v_1,...,v_N} {c^T u : U_ℓ u + V v_ℓ ≤ b_ℓ, ℓ = 1, ..., N}, and as such is computationally tractable.`
- our-relevance: Directly our priority target — the canonical exact "one block per vertex" reformulation for two-stage fixed-recourse with finite uncertainty; the theorem is about the ARC, not about the AARC, and says nothing about whether an affine rule attains it.

### thm-bgt-3: AARC of fixed-recourse LP with tractable Z is tractable
- statement (Theorem 3.1): For the fixed-recourse uncertain LP (4), if the uncertainty set Z is computationally tractable (has a separation oracle), then the AARC (8) is computationally tractable as well. Proof: with V fixed the AARC rewrites as the usual RC of an uncertain LP whose data depend affinely on ζ (displays (9)–(10)), so RC tractability results of [4] apply.
- conditions: fixed recourse matrix V; computationally tractable (separation-oracle) Z; tractability here means polynomial-time solvability via the oracle, not an explicit extended LP.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 7 (printed 357) — `Theorem 3.1. Consider the fixed recourse LP_𝒵 (4). If 𝒵 is a computationally tractable set, then the AARC (8) of this LP_𝒵 is computationally tractable as well.`
- our-relevance: Confirms that for our two-stage fixed-recourse setting the affine decision rule problem is always efficiently solvable, so the interesting question is exactly its optimality gap, not its tractability.

### thm-bgt-4: AARC with conic-representable perturbation set = explicit conic program
- statement (Theorem 3.2): Assume the perturbation set is given by a conic representation X = {ξ | ∃ω: Aξ + Bω ≥_K d} (display (15)) which is strictly feasible (there exist ξ̄, ω̄ with Aξ̄ + Bω̄ − d ∈ int K). Then the AARC of the fixed-recourse LP (4) is equivalent to the explicit conic program (16) (dual multipliers λ¹, ..., λᵐ per constraint, λⁱ ≥_{K*} 0). In particular, if K is a direct product of Lorentz cones or the semidefinite cone, the AARC is, respectively, an explicit conic quadratic or semidefinite program, of polynomial size in the description of (15) and the parameterization (11).
- conditions: fixed recourse; conic representation of the perturbation set; strict feasibility of the representation (Slater); affine dependence of the uncertainty data on ξ.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 9 (printed 359) — `Then the AARC of the fixed recourse LP (4) is equivalent to the optimization program (16)` and `In particular, in the case when K is a direct product of Lorentz cones or a semidefinite cone, the AARC of the fixed recourse LP (4) is, respectively, an explicit Conic Quadratic or Semidefinite Programming program.`
- our-relevance: The conic-duality machinery (one dual block per constraint i) is exactly the proof pattern our exactness conditions must engage with; "equivalence" here is AARC ⇔ its conic reformulation, not AARC ⇔ ARC.

### thm-bgt-5: AARC with polyhedral perturbation set = explicit LP
- statement (Theorem 3.3): If the perturbation set is polyhedral, X = {ξ | ∃ω: Aξ + Bω ≥ d} (display (18), K the nonnegative orthant), then the AARC of the fixed-recourse LP (4) is equivalent to an explicit LP; the size of this LP is polynomial in the size of the description of the perturbation set and of the parameterization mapping (12). The proof carries over from Theorem 3.2 without the strict-feasibility assumption.
- conditions: fixed recourse; polyhedral perturbation set given by linear inequalities; no Slater condition needed.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 10 (printed 360) — `Theorem 3.3. In the case of polyhedral perturbation set (18), the AARC of the fixed recourse LP (4) is equivalent to an explicit LP program. The sizes of this LP are polynomial in the sizes of the description of the perturbation set and the parametrization mapping (12).`
- our-relevance: Our priority target — this is the "AARC as a single LP" theorem we build on; note it is an exact reformulation of the AARC only, and the inventory model's LP (39) is obtained by citing it.

### thm-bgt-6: SDP conservative approximation of AARC (uncertainty-affected recourse)
- statement (Theorem 4.1): For uncertainty-affected recourse, with AARC constraints written as quadratic forms (20) and perturbation set the intersection of K concentric ellipsoids (21), the explicit semidefinite program (23) is a conservative approximation of the AARC (13): every x that can be extended, by some λ¹, ..., λᵐ, to a feasible solution of (23) is feasible for (13).
- conditions: recourse matrix V affected by uncertainty; quadratic-form rewriting of the AARC constraints; perturbation set = intersection of K ellipsoidal/cylindrical sets; approximation is one-sided (feasible set of SDP ⊆ feasible set of AARC).
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 13 (printed 363) — `is a "conservative approximation" of the AARC (13), i.e., if x can be extended, by some λ^1,...,λ^m, to a feasible solution of (23), then x is feasible for (13).`
- our-relevance: Template for one-sided quality certificates; the conservatism is with respect to the AARC, so it upper-bounds nothing about the ARC–AARC gap itself.

### thm-bgt-7: SDP (23) exactly equals AARC for a single ellipsoid
- statement (Theorem 4.2): In the case of simple ellipsoidal uncertainty (K = 1, perturbation set an ellipsoid centered at the origin), the explicit semidefinite program (23) is equivalent to the AARC (13) (no gap, via the exact S-Lemma, Lemma 4.3).
- conditions: K = 1 only; the exactness comes from the S-Lemma being lossless for one quadratic constraint; for K > 1 the approximate S-Lemma (Lemma 4.4) gives only the factor-Ω guarantee of Theorem 4.3.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 14 (printed 364) — `Theorem 4.2. In the case of simple ellipsoidal uncertainty K = 1, the explicit semidefinite program (23) is equivalent to the AARC.`
- our-relevance: The paper's only "exactness" result involving the AARC — and it is exactness of a tractable reformulation of the AARC, not equality of AARC and the true adjustable optimum; the K=1-exact / K>1-loose boundary is a structural phenomenon our exactness program should explain.

### thm-bgt-8: Tightness of the SDP approximation: factor Ω vs the true ARC
- statement (Theorem 4.3): The projection on the x-space of the feasible set of the approximate AARC (23) is contained in the feasible set of the true ARC at perturbation level ρ and contains the feasible set of the AARC at perturbation level Ωρ; accordingly, the optimal value of (23) lies between the optimal values of the AARC at levels ρ and Ωρ. Here Ω = sqrt(2 ln(6 Σ_k Rank(R_k))) (dyadic R₀) or sqrt(2 ln(16n² Σ_k Rank(R_k))) otherwise, and the authors note Ω ≤ 6 whenever the total rank of the S_k is below 65,000,000.
- conditions: uncertainty-affected recourse, K ≥ 1 ellipsoids; approximation certificate (23); constant-factor, worst-case (approximate S-Lemma) guarantee.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 16 (printed 366) — `The projection on the x-space of the feasible set of the approximate AARC (23) is contained in the feasible set of the true ARC, the perturbation level being ρ, and contains the feasible set of the AARC, the perturbation level being Ωρ.` and `it is ≤ 6, provided that the total rank of the matrices S_k participating in the description of the perturbation set is less than 65,000,000.`
- our-relevance: The only quantitative guarantee in the paper connecting a tractable surrogate to the true adjustable optimum — by perturbation inflation, not by exactness conditions; a baseline our conj-ldr-exactness results will be compared against.

## Fact blocks

### fact-bgt-1: Fixed recourse and the recourse matrix
- statement: Borrowing two-stage stochastic programming terminology, the matrix V multiplying the adjustable variables is called the recourse matrix; when V is not uncertain the uncertain LP (4) min{cᵀu : Uu + Vv ≤ b} is called a fixed recourse one. The ARC of (3) is min cᵀu over u such that ∀ζ ∈ Z ∃v : Uu + Vv ≤ b (display (5)); the RC requires one v to work for all ζ (display (6)).
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 3 (printed 353) — `Borrowing from the terminology of "Two-stage stochastic programming under uncertainty" ([10],[17]), the matrix V is called recourse matrix. When V is not uncertain, we call the corresponding uncertain LP (4) a fixed recourse one.`
- applies: All our two-stage fixed-recourse blocks; fixes the canonical notation u (non-adjustable) / v (adjustable) used across the corpus.

### fact-bgt-2: ARC can be drastically less conservative than RC
- statement: Example 1.2: the uncertain LP min{−u : αu + βv = 1} with (α, β) ∈ [½,1] × [½,1] has RC feasible set reduced to the single point u = 0 (optimal value 0), while the ARC admits u ∈ [½,1] with v(α,β) = (1−αu)/β (optimal value −1): a single "wait-and-see" variable restores feasibility the RC cannot see.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 4 (printed 354) — `It can easily be seen that the ARC is more flexible than the RC, i.e., it has a larger robust feasible set, enabling a better optimal value while still satisfying all possible realizations of the constraints.`
- applies: Motivates adaptivity and gives a tiny hand-checkable instance (uncertainty in a single constraint coefficient) for judge self-tests.

### fact-bgt-3: ARC hardness boundary in the fixed-recourse case
- statement: With fixed recourse, the ARC is an LP (hence tractable) when Z is a finite convex hull (Theorem 2.2), but it can be NP-hard when Z is a general polytope given by linear inequalities, and also when Z is a finite convex hull but the V_i depend on i; proofs are in reference [12] (Goryashko's Master's thesis, Technion 2002, not in our corpus).
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 5 (printed 355) — `Even in the fixed recourse case with a general-type polytope Z given by a list of linear inequalities, the ARC can be NP-hard. The same is true when Z is given by (A), but V_i do depend on i (for proofs of these claims, see [12]).`
- applies: Delimits where our "exact reformulation" ambitions can live (finite Z, fixed V) and where they provably cannot (uncertain recourse matrix).

### fact-bgt-4: AARC with uncertainty-affected recourse is NP-hard
- statement: Example 3.1: for min{u : u ≥ ξᵀv ≥ 0, v ≥ Qξ, −v ≥ −Qξ} over the simplex Δ, the AARC value equals max over ζ ∈ Δ of ζᵀQζ; maximizing a quadratic form over the standard simplex is equivalent to checking copositivity, which is NP-hard — so with uncertain recourse matrix even the AARC is intractable.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 10 (printed 360) — `The optimal value in the AARC is max_{ζ∈Δ}{ζ^T Q ζ}. Thus, if the AARC in question would be computationally tractable, so would be the problem of maximizing a quadratic form over the standard simplex. But the latter is equivalent to the problem of checking copositivity, a problem known to be NP-hard (see, e.g., [15, 16]).`
- applies: Justifies the fixed-recourse scope of our pipeline; also the canonical copositivity reduction to cite against any "solve the adjustable problem exactly" claim.

### fact-bgt-5: Information bases: partial affine dependence is free
- statement: Remark 4.1: everything in Sections 3–4 remains valid when each adjustable variable v_i is restricted to be affine in only a prescribed portion P_iξ of the perturbation vector (P_i given matrices) — multi-stage/incremental information structures cost nothing in the derivations.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 16 (printed 366) — `all the constructions and results of Sections 3,4 remain valid in the case when every one of the adjustable variables v_i is allowed to be an affine function of a "prescribed portion" P_iξ of the perturbation vector, where P_i are given matrices.`
- applies: Our multi-stage / causality extensions; the inventory model implements exactly this with bases I_t ⊆ {1,...,t}.

### fact-bgt-6: Empirical price of the affine restriction is small in the inventory model
- statement: In the T = 24, I = 3 inventory experiments (100 uniform-demand simulations per level), the AARC policy cost exceeded the "ideal" (hindsight-optimal) cost on average by 0.2%, 0.6%, 1.6%, 3.4% at uncertainty levels 2.5%, 5%, 10%, 20%; the RC was infeasible already at the 5% level (feasible only at 2.5%, costing 4.3% over ideal), and with an empty information basis the problem is infeasible at 20%.
- anchor: 02_BenTal_Goryashko_etal_2004.pdf, p. 22 (printed 372) — `What is surprising, is the low "price of robustness": even at the 20% uncertainty level, the average management cost for the robustly adjustable policy was just by 3.4% worse than the corresponding ideal cost; the similar quantity for the 2.5%-uncertainty in the demand was just 0.3%.` (RC infeasibility: p. 23 — `It turns out that the RC is infeasible even at the 5% uncertainty level.`)
- applies: Empirical motivation (not evidence) for conj-ldr-exactness: near-optimality observed, never proved; the same instance family can serve as a benchmark for our exactness checkers.

## Landscape
- cites (from corpus): builds directly on Ben-Tal & Nemirovski 1999 (paper refs [3], [4] — RC tractability used in the proofs of Theorems 3.1/3.2). It is the common ancestor of the affine-rule line in the corpus: Chen-Zhang 2009, Zeng-Zhao 2013, Bertsimas-Goyal-Lu 2015, Bertsimas-deRuiter 2016, Ayoub-Poss 2016, Xu-Burer 2017, Marandi-denHertog 2018, Ben-Tal-ElHousni-Goyal 2018, Bertsimas-Shtern 2018, Georghiou-Tsoukalas-Wiesemann 2020/2025, Lu-Sturt 2022, Tsang-Shehadeh-Curtis 2022.
- extends: Ben-Tal-Nemirovski 1999 (static RC) to the adjustable setting (ARC, AARC).
- contradicts-or-refines: nothing in the corpus; it *delimits* the corpus: fixed recourse + finite/ellipsoidal/polyhedral Z is the tractable-and-analyzable zone (Theorems 2.2, 3.2, 3.3, 4.2), uncertain recourse is NP-hard even under affine rules (Example 3.1). The exactness question is explicitly *not* settled here: Theorem 2.1's RC=ARC conditions are called stringent, the AARC is presented as a restriction ("the simplest restriction ... is to require of the adjustable variables to be affine functions of the data", p. 6), and the only quantitative AARC-side guarantee (Theorem 4.3) bounds the SDP surrogate, not the AARC-vs-ARC gap.

## Open threads for our portfolio
- conj-ldr-exactness: BGT-2004 contains no theorem "AARC optimal value = ARC optimal value" for fixed recourse in general. Exactness appearances are, exhaustively: (i) ARC = RC under Theorem 2.1's assumptions; (ii) ARC = LP for finite Z (Theorem 2.2) — a statement about the ARC, with per-vertex recourse variables, silent about affine rules; (iii) AARC = explicit conic/LP reformulation of itself (Theorems 3.2, 3.3); (iv) SDP surrogate = AARC for a single ellipsoid (Theorem 4.2). Our central conjecture lives precisely in the gap this paper leaves open.
- Finite-Z subtlety worth a node: Theorem 2.2's LP optimizes a separate v_ℓ per vertex; interpolating gives a piecewise-affine recourse function, not a globally affine one — so AARC = ARC for finite Z is NOT an immediate corollary and is a sharp first test case for our exactness conditions (connect to Zeng-Zhao 2013 and Bertsimas-Goyal-Lu 2015).
- Theorem 2.1's condition (2) (existence of a compact V_u per RC/ARC-feasible u) and constraint-wise uncertainty are concrete structural hypotheses to reuse or weaken in our exactness profile; the appendix proof (Karlin–Bohnenblust theorem, pp. 374–375) is the duality template.
- The K = 1 (exact S-Lemma) vs K > 1 (factor-Ω) boundary suggests exactness of tractable surrogates is tied to the number of "quadratic" uncertainty constraints — a pattern to check against Marandi-denHertog 2018 and Georghiou-Tsoukalas-Wiesemann 2020/2025.
- The inventory experiment (fact-bgt-6) is a reproducible near-exactness benchmark; a judge could recompute the ideal vs AARC gap and log exactness margins as events.
- Reference [12] (Goryashko Master's thesis, Technion 2002) carries the proofs of Theorem 2.2 and the ARC NP-hardness claims; consider adding it to corpus/MANIFEST if we ever need those proofs as anchors.
