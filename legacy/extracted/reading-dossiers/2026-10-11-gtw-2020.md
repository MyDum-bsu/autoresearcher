## Theorem blocks

### thm-gtw20-1: Proposition 1 — extreme point reformulation: exactness needs ONLY fixed recourse + affine data, never uncertainty geometry
- statement: Problem 𝒫: minimize q'x s.t. T(ξ)x + Wy(ξ) ≥ h(ξ) ∀ξ ∈ Ξ, x ∈ R^n1, y: Ξ → R^n2, with q, W constant, T and h affine, Ξ = {ξ : Fξ ≤ g} nonempty and bounded polyhedron. Let 𝒫' be the extreme point reformulation: same problem with Ξ replaced by ext Ξ and y: ext Ξ → R^n2. Then 𝒫 and 𝒫' are equivalent in the sense that any feasible solution to one can be transformed into a feasible solution to the other attaining the same objective value. Proof mechanism: for ξ ∈ Ξ, take barycentric weights λ(ξ,ζ), ζ ∈ ext Ξ, with Σ_ζ λ(ξ,ζ) = 1 and Σ_ζ λ(ξ,ζ)·ζ = ξ, and set y(ξ) = Σ_ζ λ(ξ,ζ)·y'(ζ); feasibility transfers because T is affine and W is CONSTANT, so T(ξ)x + Wy(ξ) = Σ_ζ λ(ξ,ζ)·[T(ζ)x + Wy'(ζ)] ≥ Σ_ζ λ(ξ,ζ)·h(ζ) = h(ξ).
- conditions: the whole exactness chain rests on (i) affinity of T, h in ξ and (ii) fixed recourse W. The uncertainty set needs NO structure beyond bounded polyhedrality (any geometry, any number of inequalities). The proof "does not rely on the characteristics of the first-stage decision x", so the statement immediately extends to mixed-integer here-and-now decisions (𝒫_d of Remark 1). CRITICAL FAILURE MODE: the statement does NOT hold under random recourse W(ξ) (Example 1, see fact-gtw20-2). The price: |ext Ξ| is superpolynomial in the data (dual upper bound theorem, Seidel 2004; attained by dual cyclic polytopes), and 𝒫 is NP-hard in general (Guslitzer 2002, theorem 3.4).

### thm-gtw20-2: Theorem 1 (Duality) with Propositions 2–3 — two-sided certified sandwich, AARC is the base case of both hierarchies
- statement: Primal bounds 𝒫̄(Ξ_S): scenario set Ξ_S ⊆ ext Ξ, affine set Ξ_A = conv([ext Ξ] \ Ξ_S); minimize q'x with pointwise decisions y_S on Ξ_S and an affine rule y_A on Ξ_A; reformulable as an LP polynomial in (n1, n2, m, |Ξ_S|, k, ℓ). Dual bounds 𝒟(Ξ_S): LP dual of the extreme point reformulation, dual variables Λ split into pointwise Λ_S on Ξ_S and affine Λ_A on Ξ_A; involves the moment matrix Σ_{Ξ_A} = Σ_{ξ∈ext Ξ_A}(ξ;1)(ξ;1)'. Then: (Prop 2) feasible solutions nest monotonically in Ξ_S and 𝒫̄(ext Ξ) ≡ 𝒫; (Prop 3) 𝒟(Ξ_S) nests monotonically, 𝒟(ext Ξ) is infeasible iff 𝒫 is unbounded, and if both are feasible their optimal values coincide; (Theorem 1) weak duality 𝒟(Ξ_S) ≤ 𝒫 ≤ 𝒫̄(Ξ_S) for ALL Ξ_S ⊆ ext Ξ, and strong duality 𝒟(Ξ_S) = 𝒫 = 𝒫̄(Ξ_S) at Ξ_S = ext Ξ if 𝒫 is feasible.
- conditions: Ξ_S any subset of the extreme points; the sandwich is VALID (not merely convergent) at every level; the LP reformulation of 𝒟(Ξ_S) scales polynomially in (n1, n2, m, |Ξ_S|, k, ℓ). Base cases on the primal side: "The choices Ξ_S = ∅ (and, thus, Ξ_A = Ξ) ... and Ξ_S = ext Ξ (and, thus, Ξ_A = ∅) recover the affine decision rule approximation and the extreme point reformulation 𝒫′, respectively." Base cases on the dual side: "The choice Ξ_S = ∅ (and, thus, Ξ_A = Ξ) corresponds to a highly tractable but usually suboptimal dual affine decision rule formulation, whereas Ξ_S = ext Ξ (and, thus, Ξ_A = ∅) recovers the optimal but typically intractable dual extreme point reformulation (1)." Caveat: the moment matrix Σ_A "appears difficult to compute as it involves sums of the (outer products of the) extreme points in the affine set Ξ_A"; the online appendix derives it analytically for several common uncertainty-set classes when Ξ_A = Ξ.

### thm-gtw20-3: Theorem 2 (Finite Convergence) + Observation 1 — finitely many scenario lifts turn the AARC into the exact optimum
- statement: The primal-dual lifting scheme runs a feasibility phase (minimize the worst-case constraint violation; terminates at 0 = feasible, positive = 𝒫 infeasible) and an optimality phase (terminate when 𝒟(Ξ_S) = 𝒫̄(Ξ_S)), starting from the affine bounds 𝒫̄(∅), 𝒟(∅) and iterating: select an extreme point ξ* ∈ ext Ξ_A, solve the bounding problems, and update Ξ_S ← Ξ_S ∪ {ξ*}, Ξ_A ← conv([ext Ξ_A] \ {ξ*}). Observation 1 (binding scenarios): only scenarios binding for the current affine rule need be lifted — lifting ξ* ∉ Ξ_A* does not improve 𝒫̄(Ξ_S), and once Ξ_A* = ∅ we have 𝒫̄(Ξ_S) = 𝒫̄(ext Ξ). Theorem 2: the algorithm terminates in finite time and either determines an optimal solution to 𝒫 or correctly identifies infeasibility/unboundedness; at most |ext Ξ| iterations.
- conditions: relies on convexity in x (for the binding-scenario shortcut and monotone bound updates); with discrete here-and-now decisions (Remark 1's 𝒫_d, Remark 2's dual construction via embedded min-min collapse to a MILP) Theorem 2 still holds — "Theorem 2 does not exploit any specific properties of the here-and-now decisions x" — but Observation 1 fails: "one can construct counterexamples in which lifting a nonbinding scenario results in an improved bound. Thus, in the presence of discrete here-and-now decisions, we can no longer restrict ourselves to lifting scenarios ξ* ∈ Ξ_A*." NO relatively complete recourse is required anywhere (in contrast to Zeng-Zhao 2013; Table 1). Runtime dominated by: bounding LPs, neighbor/facet enumeration of Ξ_A, and convex hulls conv N(ξ*), which "can grow exponentially (Angulo et al. 2015, proposition 5)"; optimizing a linear function over Ξ_A after a flexible number of lifts is NP-hard (Angulo et al. 2015, theorem 11).
- anchor: 23_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2020.pdf, p. 10 (printed 581) — `Theorem 2 (Finite Convergence). The algorithm terminates in finite time, and it either determines an optimal solution to 𝒫 or it correctly identifies the infeasibility or unboundedness of the problem.` and `We first show that the algorithm terminates after at most |ext Ξ| executions of the solution steps 2(i) and 3(i).` ; p. 9 (printed 580) — `Observation 1 (Binding Scenarios). For any scenario set ΞS ⊆ ext Ξ, the binding scenario set ΞA* satisfies ... i. If ξ* ∉ ΞA*, then the update ΞS ← ΞS ∪ {ξ*} and ΞA ← conv ([ext ΞA] \ {ξ*}) does not improve the objective value of the conservative approximation 𝒫̄(ΞS). ii. If ΞA* = ∅, then 𝒫̄(ΞS) = 𝒫̄(ext Ξ).` ; p. 11 (printed 582) — `Observation 1, on the other hand, crucially relies on the convexity in x, and one can construct counterexamples in which lifting a nonbinding scenario results in an improved bound.` ; p. 11 (printed 582) — `optimizing a linear function over ΞA after a flexible (i.e., not a priori fixed) number of scenarios has been lifted is NP-hard (Angulo et al. 2015, theorem 11) for generic polyhedral uncertainty sets.`
- our-relevance: This is the paper's answer to "what happens when the affine rule is NOT exact": a finite, certifiable convergence path from the AARC value to the true value, with each lifted extreme point acting as a pointwise repair of the affine rule. For our guarantee-gap claims this supplies the certified-gap technology: on any of our 18 instances, a judge can run k lifting steps and log the certified bracket 𝒟(Ξ_S) ≤ 𝒫 ≤ 𝒫̄(Ξ_S) as journal events, turning "the ADR is suboptimal by X" into a machine-checkable statement.

### thm-gtw20-4: Proposition 4 — recourse-set completion: any rule whose graph lies in conv(gr y_A ∪ gr y_S) is feasible with the same value
- statement: Define gr y = {(ξ; y(ξ)) : ξ ∈ Ξ} and rec(y_A, y_S) = conv( {(ξ; y_A(ξ)) : ξ ∈ Ξ_A} ∪ {(ξ; y_S(ξ)) : ξ ∈ Ξ_S} ). If (x, y_A, y_S) is feasible in 𝒫̄(Ξ_S), then for ANY decision rule y: Ξ → R^n2 with gr y ⊆ rec(y_A, y_S), the pair (x, y) is feasible in the TRUE problem 𝒫 and attains the same objective value as (x, y_A, y_S) in 𝒫̄(Ξ_S). Proof: ext rec(y_A, y_S) consists of graph points (ξ'; y_A(ξ')), ξ' ∈ ext Ξ_A and (ξ'; y_S(ξ')), ξ' ∈ Ξ_S; write (ξ; y(ξ)) as a convex combination via weights λ(ξ,ζ) (same recombination as Proposition 1); then T(ξ)x + Wy(ξ) = Σ λ·[T(ζ)x + Wy(·)] ≥ h(ξ), using affinity of T, h and constancy of W.
- conditions: inclusion of graphs is SUFFICIENT, not necessary — the converse fails (Example 2, fact-gtw20-4); the recourse set depends on the chosen (y_A, y_S), not only on Ξ_S; validity again requires affine T, h and fixed W.
- anchor: 23_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2020.pdf, p. 12 (printed 583) — `Proposition 4. If (x, yA, yS) is feasible in 𝒫̄(ΞS), then, for any decision rule y : Ξ → Rn2 satisfying gr y ⊆ rec (yA, yS), (x, y) is feasible in 𝒫 and attains the same objective value as (x, yA, yS) in 𝒫̄(ΞS).`
- our-relevance: A reduced-form feasibility certificate: a nonlinear (e.g., piecewise) candidate recourse for our instances is certified feasible WITHOUT checking infinitely many constraints, by membership of its graph in a single convex hull computed from finitely many affine-rule + scenario values. This is the constructive bridge from "affine + finitely many pointwise fixes" to arbitrary decision rules — the same shape as our "full-information formula + wedge" story but with convexity replacing inverse-positivity.

### thm-gtw20-5: Proposition 5 + completion — piecewise affine decision rules = simplicial decompositions; barycentric closed form per simplex
- statement: Fix Ξ_S ⊆ ext Ξ and (y_A, y_S). (i) Every piecewise affine decision rule y (affine on each cell of a finite polyhedral partition) with gr y ⊆ rec(y_A, y_S) is affine on each simplex of some simplicial decomposition {Ξ_ℓ} of Ξ; (ii) conversely, for every simplicial decomposition {Ξ_ℓ} there is a rule y with gr y ⊆ rec(y_A, y_S), affine over each Ξ_ℓ — explicitly, display (4): on each simplex Ξ_ℓ, y(ξ) = Σ_{ζ ∈ ext Ξ_ℓ} Λ(ξ,ζ)·y(ζ), where Λ(ξ,ζ) are the barycentric coordinates of ξ in Ξ_ℓ (corner points ⊔_ℓ ext Ξ_ℓ). Consequence: "Propositions 4 and 5 allow us to complete any feasible solution (x, y_A, y_S) in problem 𝒫̄(Ξ_S) to a feasible solution (x, y) in problem 𝒫 that attains the same objective value and that is piecewise affine over any fixed simplicial decomposition {Ξ_ℓ} of Ξ"; compact descriptions via triangulation schemes (Büeler et al. 2000): the affine rule over cl(Ξ_ℓ \ Ξ_S) is derived from the affine rule on Ξ_A extended over the simplex's corner neighborhood N(ξ).
- conditions: the rule is completed AFTER solving the bounding problem (ex-post reconstruction, not an optimization over piecewise rules); simplex cells use extreme points of Ξ as corners; gr y ⊆ rec(y_A, y_S) is preserved because barycentric combinations of feasible graph points stay feasible (Proposition 4); the statement of Proposition 5(i) presumes the piecewise rule already satisfies the graph inclusion.
- anchor: 23_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2020.pdf, pp. 12–13 (printed 583–584) — `Proposition 5. Consider a scenario set ΞS ⊆ ext Ξ as well as the second-stage decision yA : ΞA → Rn2 and yS : ΞS → Rn2. Then 1. For every piecewise affine decision rule y : Ξ → Rn2 with gr y ⊆ rec(yA, yS), there is a simplicial decomposition {Ξℓ}ℓ of Ξ such that y is affine on every simplex Ξℓ. 2. For every simplicial decomposition {Ξℓ}ℓ of Ξ, there is a decision rule y : Ξ → Rn2 with gr y ⊆ rec(yA, yS) such that y is affine over Ξℓ.` ; p. 13 (printed 584) — `Propositions 4 and 5 allow us to complete any feasible solution (x, yA, yS) in problem 𝒫̄(ΞS) to a feasible solution (x, y) in problem 𝒫 that attains the same objective value and that is piecewise affine over any fixed simplicial decomposition {Ξℓ}ℓ of Ξ.` ; display (4) (barycentric rule) on p. 13 (printed 584).
- our-relevance: CLOSEST REDUCED-FORM RESULT to our full-information formulas in this paper: a cell-wise CLOSED FORM y(ξ) = Σ Λ(ξ,ζ)y(ζ) — linear in ξ per cell, values at corners carrying the "full information" of each region, exact because the data are affine on the cell. Compare GTW-2025's formula (17) y^ℓ(ξ) = B_I^{-1}[g_I(ξ) − A_I(ξ)x]: that one needs an M-matrix; the barycentric one needs only affinity + fixed W. Our wedge counterexamples should be tested against simplicial decompositions: a wedge where binding sets rotate is exactly a simplex boundary, and the barycentric formula predicts exactness with K cells = vertices of the decomposition — the LP-geometry analogue of the 2025 K-adaptability bound K ≥ K_A·K_B.

### thm-gtw20-6: Remark 2 + Remark 4 — the mixed-integer here-and-now extension: convergence survives, binding-scenario shortcut does not
- statement: For 𝒫_d with x_d ∈ Z^{nd} (continuous x_c alongside), Remark 2 derives a convergent hierarchy of dual bounds: for fixed x_d the embedded minimization over x_c is replaced by its LP dual, and robust-optimization reformulation + strong LP duality in an embedded minimization collapse the min-min problem into a single-stage mixed-integer linear program; the same works for the feasibility variant. Remark 4: Theorem 2's finite convergence carries over verbatim ("does not exploit any specific properties of the here-and-now decisions x"), but Observation 1's restriction to binding scenarios relies on convexity in x — counterexamples exist where lifting a NONBINDING scenario improves the bound, so with discrete x_d one must consider all extreme points as lift candidates.
- conditions: discrete decisions in the FIRST stage only; second stage stays continuous linear; the dual-side reformulation needs the conic/linear description of Ξ_A and moment-matrix updates.
- anchor: 23_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2020.pdf, p. 7 (printed 578) — `Perhaps surprisingly, we can derive a convergent hierarchy of dual bounds to the mixed-integer, two-stage robust optimization problem 𝒫d as well.` ; p. 11 (printed 582) — `Observation 1, on the other hand, crucially relies on the convexity in x, and one can construct counterexamples in which lifting a nonbinding scenario results in an improved bound. ### fact-gtw20-1: Problem class, Table 1 guarantees, and where this scheme sits among solution schemes

### fact-gtw20-2: Example 1 (random recourse) — the exactness chain collapses without fixed W
- statement: minimize 0 s.t. ξ·y(ξ) ≥ 1 ∀ξ ∈ [−1,1], y: [−1,1] → R. The problem 𝒫 is INFEASIBLE (any y violates 0·y(0) ≥ 1), but its extreme point reformulation is solved by any y with y(−1) ≤ −1 and y(1) ≥ 1 — feasibility of 𝒫' no longer implies feasibility of 𝒫 once W depends on ξ.
- anchor: 23_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2020.pdf, p. 4 (printed 575) — `This problem is infeasible because any second-stage decision y violates the constraint 0 · y(0) ≥ 1, but its extreme point formulation is solved by any y satisfying y(−1) ≤ −1 and y(1) ≥ 1.`
- our-relevance: Sharp audit point: every exactness claim for our instances (both the GTW-2020 recombination mechanism and, presumably, our own judges' certificates) silently uses fixed recourse; an instance with ξ-dependent recourse coefficients invalidates the extreme-point/barycentric machinery wholesale. Our 18-instance family should be audited for accidental ξ-dependence of the recourse matrix.

### fact-gtw20-3: The dual affine bound dominates the Hadjiyiannis sampling bound; moment matrix is the computational bottleneck
- statement: The sampling bound (SB) of Hadjiyiannis et al. 2011 — ADR problem restricted to a fixed scenario subset Ξ_S — has an LP dual that is exactly an instance of 𝒟(Ξ_S) with Λ_A ≡ 0 on Ξ_A; hence 𝒟(Ξ_S) is at least as tight as SB for the same Ξ_S. The cost of the dual bound is the moment matrix Σ_A (sums of extreme points and their outer products over ext Ξ_A); the online appendix gives closed forms for common uncertainty-set classes when Ξ_A = Ξ, and per-iteration updates of Σ_A need only the outer product of the removed scenario ξ* ∈ ext Ξ_A.
- anchor: 23_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2020.pdf, p. 8 (printed 579) — `The dual associated with this problem corresponds to an instance of our dual bound 𝒟(ΞS), where we fix λA (ξ) = 0, ξ ∈ ΞA, and only optimize over λS. Thus, our bound 𝒟(ΞS) is at least as tight as the bound of Hadjiyiannis et al. (2011).` ; p. 11 (printed 582) — `Finally, the initial moment matrix ΣA can often be computed efficiently—see the online appendix—and updating ΣA only requires subtracting the (outer product of the) scenario ξ* ∈ ΞA that is removed in each iteration.`
- our-relevance: Our pipeline currently has no lower bound on the true adjustable value; 𝒟(∅) (dual affine rule) is a one-LP certified lower bound that strictly improves on the sampling-bound logic we discussed around BGT-2004's empirical gap numbers — a drop-in judge for certified gap reporting.

### fact-gtw20-4: Example 2 — optimal decision rules can lie OUTSIDE every recourse set of the lifting hierarchy
- statement: minimize x s.t. x ≥ y(ξ), y(ξ) ≥ ξ, y(ξ) ≥ −ξ, ∀ξ ∈ [−1,1]. For EVERY scenario set Ξ_S ⊆ [−1,1], the unique optimal solution (x, y_A, y_S) of 𝒫̄(Ξ_S) satisfies x = 1 and rec(y_A, y_S) = [−1,1] × {1}; yet the problem is optimized by x = 1 and any rule y ≡ const ∈ [ξ,1] pointwise (e.g., y(ξ) = |ξ| ≤ 1), whose graph is NOT contained in that recourse set. So gr y ⊆ rec(y_A, y_S) is strictly weaker than "y is optimal": feasible solutions (x, y) of 𝒫 can escape rec(y_A, y_S) for every conservative approximation simultaneously.
- anchor: 23_Georghiou_Tsoukalas_Wiesemann_2020.pdf, p. 12 (printed 583) — `Example 2. Consider the two-stage robust optimization problem` (display (3): `minimize x subject to x ≥ y(ξ), y(ξ) ≥ ξ, y(ξ) ≥ −ξ ∀ξ ∈ [−1,1]`) `For every scenario set ΞS ⊆ [−1, 1], the unique optimal solution (x, yA, yS) to 𝒫̄(ΞS) satisfies x = 1 and rec(yA, yS) = [−1,1] × {1}. Problem (3) is optimized, however, by x = 1 and every decision rule y : [−1,1] → R+ satisfying y(ξ) ∈ [ξ, 1].`
- our-relevance: Mirrors GTW-2025's formulation-sensitivity warnings (their fact blocks on epigraph reformulation): the lifting hierarchy certifies optimality of the x-part and of SPECIFIC completed rules, and its recourse set does not characterize all optimal rules — a completeness caveat when our judges equate "certified optimal value attained" with "the formula we reconstructed is the optimal rule".

### fact-gtw20-5: Numerical exactness profiles — how many lifts to exactness, and how much of the ADR gap they close

## Landscape
- Relation to BGT-2004 (PRIORITY TARGET 2): GTW-2020 subsumes the AARC as 𝒫̄(∅) — the base case of the primal hierarchy — and adds its missing dual mirror 𝒟(∅). BGT-2004's Theorem 3.3 (AARC as explicit LP for polyhedral Z) is the workhorse for the LP reformulation of 𝒫̄(Ξ_S) (same Guslitzer 2002 duality route, cited for it); BGT-2004's Theorem 2.2 (ARC = one recourse block per vertex for finite Z) is the special case of the extreme point reformulation with |ext Ξ| finite. Crucially, GTW-2020 inherits BGT-2004's honesty: the affine layer is presented as "usually suboptimal", and unlike BGT-2004's factor-Ω SDP bounds, the GTW-2020 sandwich is EXACT at every finite level (valid brackets, monotonically tightening, finite convergence) — the certified successor of the BGT-2004 approximation layer.
- Relation to GTW-2025 (our condition (B) paper) and the 2025 condition (B) = positive-inverse M-matrix + single global index set: the two papers by the same authors answer COMPLEMENTARY questions. GTW-2025: WHEN is the base case 𝒫̄(∅) already exact (constraint-algebra conditions (F),(A),(D),(B), pointwise optimality, K-adaptability repairs under coverings (A′)/(B′))? GTW-2020: GIVEN that the base case is not exact, how do we certify and close the gap finitely (extreme point lifts, dual bounds, piecewise affine completion)? The correspondence at the object level: a lifted scenario ξ* is a pointwise fix at one extreme point = one atom of a (B′)-style covering refinement; Theorem 2 = an adaptive, certifying version of the 2025 K-adaptability bound with K = number of lifts; the barycentric formula (4) is the LP-geometry analogue of the 2025 closed-form (17) — exact by affinity + fixed W instead of by inverse-positivity. Neither paper's exactness mechanism implies the other's.
- Position among the rest of the corpus: uses Bertsimas-de Ruiter 2016 only as a candidate replacement bound (tested, "did not lead to significant improvements", p. 17/588); Zeng-Zhao 2013 C&CG is the main empirical competitor, distinguished by requiring relatively complete recourse; Hadjiyiannis et al. 2011 sampling bound is strictly dominated by 𝒟(Ξ_S) (Remark 3); Gorissen-den Hertog 2015 supplies the TOY2 benchmark; Angulo et al. 2015 supplies the complexity limits of lifted formulations. The DRO strand (Xu-Burer 2017, Ben-Tal-ElHousni-Goyal 2018) is untouched here — the paper is pure robust (worst-case over Ξ, no risk/ambiguity layer), so our bridge to guaranteed control cannot quote these bounds directly without a worst-case translation.
- Proof technology: LP duality + polyhedral geometry (extreme points, moment matrices, barycentric coordinates, convex hulls of vertex neighborhoods); no M-matrix/inverse-positivity arguments anywhere — a genuinely different exactness mechanism from the 2025 paper.
