meta: "On the Sparsity of Optimal Linear Decision Rules for a Class of Robust Optimization Problems with Box Uncertainty Sets" — Haihao Lu (MIT) & Bradley Sturt (UIC). Venue: Management Science (INFORMS `mnsc` documentclass, blind-review template; HISTORY: "First version: Mar. 24, 2022. TeX: `corpus/tex_src/11_Lu_Sturt_2022/main_final.tex` (2071 lines; `.bbl` used for citations only).

Scope note: the paper does NOT study exactness of affine rules vs. full adjustability. Its object is the *parameter complexity* of optimal linear decision rules (LDRs) inside the LDR class for the Ben-Tal et al. 2004 production-inventory problem (and extensions): there always exists an optimal LDR with only O(ET) nonzero parameters, plus a reformulation + active-set algorithm exploiting this. The affine-policy LP and its dual, extreme points of the LDR epigraph polyhedron, and an exact optimality certificate are the load-bearing machinery — directly adjacent to our LP-certificate and reduced-form lines.

## Theorem blocks

### thm-ls-1: main sparsity bound for production-inventory LDRs (Theorem `thm:main`)
- statement: For the production-inventory cost function (1a)-(1d) of Ben-Tal et al. 2004 under Assumption 1 (LDR feasible with finite optimum; box uncertainty sets with ζ_1 = 1, D_1 = D̄_1), if c_te > 0 for all t, e and δ = min_e δ_e is the minimum lead time, then there exists an optimal solution ȳ of (LDR) with ‖ȳ‖_0 ≤ 2 + 8E + 10T + 6E(T−δ) = O(ET), versus ½ET(T+1) = O(ET²) total LDR parameters.
- conditions: continuous decisions, polyhedral (affine-in-ζ) constraints, separable box uncertainty sets, positive production costs; existence of an optimal LDR assumed (Assumption 1a). Proof: optimal extreme point of the LDR epigraph LP exists (lem:1), every extreme point is the unique solution of a HARD/EASY system (lem:2), reformulation into system (S-1)-(S-3) with |I| ≤ 1 + 4E + 5T + 3ET − 3Eδ (line 1332) and lem:zeros gives ‖ȳ‖_0 ≤ 2|I|.
- anchor: main_final.tex:661 — `Consider a cost function of the form \eqref{prob:example1:a}-\eqref{prob:example1:d} and let   Assumption~\ref{ass:1} hold. Suppose that $c_{te}>0$ ... Then there exists an optimal solution $\bar{\by}$ for \eqref{prob:ldr} that satisfies $\| \bar{\by} \|_0 \le  2 + 8E + 10T + 6E(T-\delta).$`
- our-relevance: for the canonical BGT-2004 two-stage/multistage ARO instance class it proves the optimal LDR has a reduced representation of the same order as the static rule — the reduced-form/compression question we pose for two-stage ARO, answered affirmatively at the level of parameter counting, not value (exactness is not claimed).

### thm-ls-2: the LDR epigraph feasible set is a polyhedron with an extreme point (Lemma `lem:1`)
- statement: Under Assumptions 1 and 2 (feasible decisions are nonnegative), the set of feasible solutions of the epigraph formulation (LDR-G) is a nonempty polyhedron with at least one extreme point. Consequently (line 713, via Bertsimas-Tsitsiklis Thm 2.7), whenever the LP has a finite optimum, an optimal solution that is an extreme point exists.
- conditions: box (interval) uncertainty sets with ζ_1 ≡ 1; finiteness of the LDR optimum; decision nonnegativity enforced by the constraints. Proof by contradiction: any lineality direction d must satisfy Σ_s d_{t,s} ζ_s = 0 for all ζ in the box, which forces d = 0.
- anchor: main_final.tex:711 — `Let Assumptions~\ref{ass:1} and~\ref{ass:2} hold. Then  the set of feasible solutions to \eqref{prob:ldr_b} is a nonempty polyhedron with at least one extreme point.`
- our-relevance: this is the entry ticket for basis/extreme-point analysis of the affine-policy LP — the same polyhedron our LP-certificate judges must characterize; it shows box uncertainty plus ζ_1 = 1 makes the affine-policy LP pointed, which is what makes optimal bases (and their active-constraint fingerprints) well-defined.

### thm-ls-3: extreme points = unique solutions of a HARD/EASY active-constraint system (Lemma `lem:2`)
- statement: Let Assumption 1 hold and (ȳ, c̄_0) be an extreme point of the feasible set of (LDR-G). Then there exist an index set I^ȳ ⊆ {0,…,m}, per-constraint stage sets T_i^ȳ ⊆ [T], and hyperplanes (α_i^ȳ, β_i^ȳ) such that ȳ is the unique solution of: (HARD) Σ_s Σ_t α_{i,t,s}^ȳ y_{t,s} = β_i^ȳ for i ∈ I^ȳ (≤ m+1 equations, hyperplanes depending on ȳ), and (EASY) Σ_t a_{i,t} y_{t,s} = b_{i,s} for i ∈ I^ȳ, s ∈ T_i^ȳ (≤ (m+1)T equations, ȳ-independent).
- conditions: box uncertainty sets; extreme point = basic feasible solution, so active constraints with D_s^* ∈ {D_s, D̄_s} chosen per the sign of (−b_{i,s} + Σ_t a_{i,t}^T ȳ_{t,s}) pin it down; s ∈ T_i^= (zero-coefficient stages) generate the EASY equalities.
- anchor: main_final.tex:728 — `&& \sum_{t=s}^T \ba_{i,t} \cdot \by_{t,s} &=  b_{i,s} && \forall i \in \mathcal{I}^{\bar{\by}}, s \in \mathcal{T}_i^{\bar{\by}}. \tag{EASY} \label{line:type2}`
- our-relevance: an optimality-condition template: the optimal affine rule is pinned by few ȳ-dependent (HARD) plus many per-stage problem-intrinsic (EASY) equalities — exactly the shape of a reduced-form/optimality-certificate characterization of two-stage ARO affine solutions that our judges could verify instance-by-instance.

### thm-ls-4: combinatorial sparsity lemma for two-block systems (Lemma `lem:zeros`)
- statement: Let p1 ∈ R^{m1×n}, p2 ∈ R^{m2×n}, q ∈ R^{m1}, N ⊆ [n] with m1, m2 ≤ n. If z̄ ∈ R^n is the unique solution of p1 z = q (S-1), p2 z = 0 (S-2), z_j = 0 for j ∈ N (S-3), and each column of p2 has at most one nonzero entry, then ‖z̄‖_0 ≤ 2m_1.
- conditions: uniqueness of the solution; column-sparsity ≤ 1 of the "easy" block p2 (each variable appears in at most one S-2 equation); no rank assumptions needed. Proof: eliminate one variable per p2-row by substitution; remaining unique solution of m1 equations has ≤ m1 nonzeros, plus ≤ m1 more from substituted variables.
- anchor: main_final.tex:752 — `Then the unique solution $\bar{\bz}$ has at most $2 m_1$ nonzero entries, that is, $\| \bar{\bz} \|_0 \le 2m_1$.`
- our-relevance: the reusable core: if the active-constraint system of an affine solution decomposes into few coupled equations plus a column-sparse per-stage block, the solution is sparse — a transferable lemma for our reduced forms whenever the two-stage ARO constraint matrix has this "one variable per easy row" structure.

### thm-ls-5: compact reformulation size for a fixed active set (Proposition `prop:productioninventory_reformulation` with Lemma `lem:reform`)
- statement: For the production-inventory problem with zero lead times, the number of unique per-stage tuples K^A = Σ_s K^{A,s} satisfies K^A ≤ 4|A| + ET + 5T + E + 1. Lemma `lem:reform` shows the standard robust-counterpart LP (P-A', with 2mT auxiliary variables) admits an optimal solution in which auxiliary variables of constraints mapping to the same tuple coincide, so (LDR-A) can be rewritten as the LP (P-A) with 1 + |A| + 2K^A variables and 1 + m + K^A constraints — i.e., imposing sparsity induces redundancy among dual auxiliary variables that can be merged.
- conditions: active set A ⊆ {(t,s,j): s ≤ t}; box uncertainty (strong duality per-stage min-form of max_ζ); duplicate-tuple structure of the production-inventory constraint family.
- anchor: main_final.tex:831 — `For the production-inventory problem with no lead times ($\delta_e = 0$ for all $e \in [E]$), we have  $ K^{\mathcal{A}} \le  4 | \mathcal{A}| + ET + 5T + E + 1$.`
- our-relevance: a concrete reduced form: combining thm-ls-1 with this, an optimal LDR is computable from an LP with O(TE) variables/constraints once the support is known — the "known-support ⇒ compact exact LP" pattern our two-stage ARO reduced forms aim for, here with the support discovered by the active-set loop.

### thm-ls-6: exact termination certificate for the active set method (Proposition `prop:terminate`, with Proposition `prop:monotone`)
- statement: Let (λ, ζ^{A,s}) be any optimal solution of the dual LP (D-A). If it satisfies Σ_{i=0}^m a_{i,t,j} · (λ_i / Σ_{i′: π(i′)=π(i)} λ_{i′}) · ζ^{A,s}_{π(i),s} = 0 for all (t,s,j) ∉ A (with 0/0 := 0), then the LDR extracted from the KKT solution of (D-A) is an optimal solution of the full (LDR). Complement (`prop:monotone`): enlarging A by any subset of the tuples satisfying the equality leaves the optimal objective of (LDR-A ∪ Ã) unchanged.
- conditions: box uncertainty; Assumption 1 (so (D) optimal value = (LDR) optimal value, and (D-A) feasible by Lemma `lem:D_A_feas`, line 1933); the certificate is checked on the excluded tuples only.
- anchor: main_final.tex:927 — ` \sum_{i=0}^m   a_{i,t,j} \left( \frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_{i' \in \{0,\ldots,m\}: \pi^{\mathcal{A},s}(i') = \pi^{\mathcal{A},s}(i)} \lambda_{i'}} \right) \zeta^{\mathcal{A},s}_{\pi^{\mathcal{A},s}(i),s} = 0 \quad \forall (t,s,j) \notin \mathcal{A}, \label{line:terminate}`
- our-relevance: this is an exact LP-duality optimality certificate for affine decision rules — dual feasibility plus objective match on the omitted coordinates — precisely the kind of checkable statement a code-judge in `judges/` can verify to certify (or refute) global optimality of a candidate LDR; no LLM judgment involved.

### thm-ls-7: sparsity for the dynamic newsvendor problem (Theorem `thm:3`)
- statement: For the single-factory dynamic newsvendor problem with convex piecewise-linear holding/backorder costs (2a)-(2b) under Assumption 1, there exists an optimal solution ȳ of (LDR) with ‖ȳ‖_0 ≤ 10 + 12T over all periods — i.e., an average of 12 + 10/T = O(1) nonzero parameters per period. Combined with Bertsimas-Iancu-Parrilo Thm 3.1 (affine policies are optimal for this class, cited at line 1047), the optimal policy uses an average of O(1) past demand realizations.
- conditions: box uncertainty; nonlinear convex cost linearized via auxiliary affine rules z_t (so the LP is in (y, w) jointly); Assumption 1. Proof: same 3-step machinery, with substitution w_{t+1,s} ← Σ h_t y_{ℓ,s} − h_t eliminating the auxiliary block, then lem:zeros.
- anchor: main_final.tex:1049 — `Consider a cost function of the form \eqref{prob:example3:a}-\eqref{prob:example3:b} and let   Assumption~\ref{ass:1} hold. Then there exists an optimal solution $\bar{\by}$ for \eqref{prob:ldr} that satisfies $\| \bar{\by} \|_0 \le 10+12T.$`
- our-relevance: on a class where affine is provably EXACT (Bertsimas-Iancu-Parrilo 2010), the optimal rule is additionally O(1)-sparse per period — a positive case linking our exactness line (when is affine optimal) with our reduced-form line (how compactly can the exact rule be stored).

### thm-ls-8: sparsity under non-separable budget uncertainty via dualization (Theorem `thm:nonbox`, with Lemmas `lem:equi`, `lem:extreme_point`)
- statement: If (LDR-4) — production-inventory with zero lead times and the non-separable budget set U = {ζ: ζ_1 = 1, D_s ≤ ζ_s ≤ D̄_s, Σ_{s=2}^{T+1}(ζ_s − D_s)/(D̄_s − D_s) ≤ T−k} — is feasible with finite optimum, there exists an optimal ȳ with ‖ȳ‖_0 ≤ 2 + 8E + 10T + 6ET + 12kT. Mechanism: Y = ∪_{μ≥0} Y_μ (dualize the budget constraint, Lemma `lem:equi`), every extreme point of Y is an extreme point of some Y_μ (Lemma `lem:extreme_point`), and Y_μ has box uncertainty.
- conditions: feasible finite problem; k small (bound degrades with kT; the authors state it is open whether sparsity holds for large k, line 1085); only one coupling row is dualized per constraint.
- anchor: main_final.tex:1081 — `If \eqref{prob:ldr_4} is feasible and has a finite optimal objective value, then there exists an optimal solution $\bar{\by}$ for \eqref{prob:ldr_4} that satisfies $\| \bar{\by} \|_0 \le  2 + 8E + 10T + 6ET + 12kT.$`
- our-relevance: template for reducing non-box (coupled) uncertainty to box form while preserving the relevant extreme-point structure — the same dualization-with-extreme-point-preservation move our reduced forms of two-stage ARO use when a coupling constraint is lifted into the objective with a multiplier.

## Fact blocks

### fact-ls-1: standing assumptions (Assumptions 1 and 2)
- statement: Assumption 1: (a) (LDR) is feasible with finite optimal value; (b) uncertainty sets are intervals U_t = [D_t, D̄_t] with D_1 = D̄_1 = 1 and D_t < D̄_t for t ≥ 2 (the ζ_1 ≡ 1 convention lets LDRs carry a nonzero offset). Assumption 2: any finite-cost decision profile satisfies x_1,…,x_T ≥ 0.
- anchor: main_final.tex:650 — `The uncertainty sets are intervals  $\mathcal{U}_t=[\ubar{D}_t, \bar{D}_t]$ with $\ubar{D}_1 = \bar{D}_1 = 1$ `
- applies: the exact profile (point mass at ζ_1 = 1 + full-dimensionality in later stages) under which the LDR polyhedron is pointed; any port of these results to our pipeline must keep this normalization.

### fact-ls-2: optimal extreme point availability for the affine LP
- statement: (LDR-G) is a linear optimization problem (linear objective, polyhedral feasible region), so if it has an extreme point and finite optimum, an optimal extreme-point solution exists (standard LP theory, cited to Bertsimas-Tsitsiklis Thm 2.7); moreover c̄_0 is uniquely determined by ȳ at an extreme point (footnote, line 714).
- anchor: main_final.tex:713 — `Therefore, whenever this linear optimization problem has at least one extreme point and has a finite optimal objective value, there must exist an optimal solution for \eqref{prob:ldr_b} that is an extreme point of its feasible set (see, e.g.,  \cite[Theorem 2.7]{bertsimas1997introduction}).`
- applies: the general license to analyze optimal affine rules via bases/extreme points instead of values — the bridge between our LP-certificate line and structural (reduced-form) statements.

### fact-ls-3: full dual LP (D) of the LDR problem and its status
- statement: The robust-counterpart dual of (LDR) for box uncertainty is LP (D): maximize −Σ_{i≥1} c_i λ_i − Σ_{i,s} b_{i,s} ζ_{i,s} s.t. Σ_i a_{i,t,j} ζ_{i,s} = 0 (s ≤ t), D_s λ_i ≤ ζ_{i,s} ≤ D̄_s λ_i, λ_0 = 1, λ_i ≥ 0; its optimal value equals that of (LDR), and opt(D) ≤ opt(D-A) for every active set A.
- anchor: main_final.tex:1759 — `The optimal objective value of \eqref{prob:D} is equal to the optimal objective value of \eqref{prob:ldr}, which implies that the optimal objective value of~\eqref{prob:D} is always less than or equal to the optimal objective value of \eqref{prob:D_A}.`
- applies: the LP-duality sandwich (D) ≤ (D-A) ≤ (LDR) with equality iff termination holds — the certificate chain a judge can compute numerically for affine-rule optimality on box-uncertainty instances.

### fact-ls-4: dualization of one coupling row preserves extreme points
- statement: For a feasible region Y defined with one extra coupling inequality f^T ζ ≤ g in the max, Y = ∪_{μ≥0} Y_μ where Y_μ adds μ(g − f^T ζ) into the i-th constraint; and every extreme point of Y is an extreme point of Y_μ for some μ ≥ 0 (weak duality one way, strong duality back).
- anchor: main_final.tex:1560 — `For any extreme point $(\by,c_0)$ of $\mathcal{Y}$, there exists $\bmu\ge \bzero$, such that $(\by,c_0)$ is an extreme point of $\mathcal{Y}_{\bmu}$.`
- applies: reduced-form device: one non-separable row can be absorbed at the price of one multiplier per constraint; sparsity/counting arguments for box sets then carry over with an additive penalty (the 12kT in thm-ls-8).

### fact-ls-5: Markovian active set and its suboptimality
- statement: The algorithm initializes A with the "Markovian active set" {(t,1,j)} ∪ {(t,t,j)} (rules of the form x_t = y_{t1} + y_{tt} ζ_t); numerically, such Markovian-sparse rules are not optimal in general — the active-set objective strictly decreases over iterations (Fig. 2 discussion).
- anchor: main_final.tex:1011 — `First, we observe from Figure~\ref{fig:active_set_fixed_T} that  sparse linear decision rules with the  Markovian active set (see \S\ref{sec:algorithm:activeset})  are not optimal in general, as the objective value of the linear decision rules found by the active set method decreases as the number of iterations increases.`
- applies: a caution for any of our heuristics that a priori restricts LDR support to offset-plus-current-stage; also evidence the optimal support is genuinely non-Markovian in this class.

### fact-ls-6: empirical sparsity matches the O(ET) bound
- statement: In the BGT-2004-derived experiments (seasonal demand, T up to a year discretized twice per week, E = 3,4,5), the optimal LDR obtained by primal simplex has ≈ 3% nonzero parameters at large T; the nonzero count grows linearly in T and stays in the narrow band between the static-rule count (lower bound) and the thm-ls-1 upper bound (multiplicative gap roughly 11).
- anchor: main_final.tex:2026 — `the number of nonzero parameters in the optimal linear decision rules  decreases to $3\%$ of the total number of parameters when inventory levels and production decisions are made twice per week over a selling horizon of one year.`
- applies: the theoretical bound is tight up to a constant factor and the linear-in-T growth is observed; supports treating sparsity as a real property of optimal LDRs, not an artifact (also supports that the count does not grow sublinearly, line 2028).

### fact-ls-7: computational scale and speedups
- statement: With the compact reformulation + active set method at T = 240, E = 5: >170x faster for LDRs within 10% of optimal and >32x faster within 1%, vs. Gurobi barrier on the robust counterpart (run without crossover); for T = 240, E = 50 the robust counterpart LP needs 13,281,993 variables, 5,936,502 constraints, 250,895,943 nonzeros — beyond classic LP solvers; PDLP/HiGHS also fails (degenerate dual, >10⁶ iterations).
- anchor: main_final.tex:1007 — `our approach is over 170x faster  in computing linear decision rules that are within 10\% of optimal, and over 32x faster in computing linear decision rules that are within 1\% of optimal.`
- applies: quantitative evidence that exploiting structure (sparsity + support identification) beats monolithic LP solving for affine rules at scale; relevant sizing data if our pipeline ever benchmarks LDR computation on this class.

### fact-ls-8: finite convergence and practical safeguards of the active set loop
- statement: With the Elzinga-Moore "Deletion Rule II" removal strategy, the active set method is guaranteed to converge to an optimal LDR after finitely many iterations; (D-A) is always feasible under Assumption 1 (feasible set of D-A ⊇ feasible set of D); unboundedness is handled by bounding λ_i ≤ M with growing M; the termination check costs O(T(Z + nK^A)) time and O(Z + nT²) memory (Z = # nonzeros of the a_{i,t}).
- anchor: main_final.tex:961 — `It is established by \cite{elzinga1975central} that the active set method with this removal strategy is guaranteed to converge to an optimal solution for \eqref{prob:ldr} after finitely many iterations.`
- applies: the algorithmic wrapper is deterministic and finite — compatible with our judge-reproducibility requirements (byte-identical reruns on frozen instances) if implemented deterministically.

## Open threads for our portfolio

1. Reusable exact certificate (thm-ls-6 + fact-ls-3): the (D) ≤ (D-A) chain with the line-(terminate) equality is a fully mechanical global-optimality certificate for an affine rule on box-uncertainty instances. Candidate judge fixture: given a frozen two-stage ARO instance and a candidate LDR, compute/verify the certificate; a failing certificate with the violating tuple (t,s,j) is a witness of affine suboptimality. This fits `judges/` with deterministic re-runs.
2. Affine-exactness vs. affine-sparsity are orthogonal: the paper never claims LDR = fully adaptive. For BGT-2004's production-inventory class the exactness question (is the affine rule optimal among all policies) remains separate; our conj-ldr-exactness should cite this paper only for the complexity side, not as an exactness source.
3. HARD/EASY decomposition (thm-ls-3) as an optimality-condition schema: our optimality-conditions line can ask whether two-stage ARO optimal affine solutions always admit a similar "few solution-dependent + many problem-intrinsic" active-constraint characterization, and whether that characterization can be turned into a constructive reduced form (support or equations) rather than only an existence bound.
4. Column-sparsity mechanism (thm-ls-4): the sparsity engine is purely combinatorial (each variable in ≤ 1 easy equation). Portfolio question: which two-stage ARO problem families (beyond inventory/newsvendor) yield column-sparse EASY blocks after the leading-1 normalization of the coupling row — a checkable structural predicate for a reduced-form judge.
5. Dualization with extreme-point preservation (fact-ls-4): a template for our reduced forms under non-separable sets; open cost accounting when multiple coupling rows are dualized (the 12kT penalty grows linearly in the number of dualized late-stage constraints — does a better bound exist?).
6. Authors' own open problems: broadest class with sparse optimal LDRs (line 1028); sparsity for large budget parameter k "as is typically recommended in the literature" (line 1085); combining sparsity with Fourier-Motzkin elimination (line 1092, pointing at Zhen-denHertog-Sim 2018) — the last one directly connects to our reduced-form pipeline.
7. Negative design guidance (fact-ls-5): any support heuristic must not be Markovian-by-construction; our algorithmic proposals for LDR computation should include a support-growing step with an exact termination test (cf. thm-ls-6) rather than a fixed pattern.
8. Boundary between the two counting regimes: sparsity bound 2 + 8E + 10T + 6E(T−δ) improves as minimum lead time δ grows (line 669, converging to O(E+T)) — a concrete, testable trade-off between instance geometry (lead times) and reduced-form size that our reduced-form experiments could replicate.
