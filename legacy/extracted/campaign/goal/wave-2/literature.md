## IY: exact assumptions and conclusions

Physical p3 defines finite-horizon discrete/countable state and discrete action sets; conditional probability ambiguity is associated with state-action pairs. Physical p4 defines histories and randomized history-dependent rules and states **Assumption 1 (Rectangularity)**: measures consistent with a policy are products of the stage conditional-measure choices. Physical p5 explains that choosing a conditional distribution at one state-action pair does not limit future choices; the criterion is worst expected reward, optimized over policies.

**Theorem 1 (Bellman equation), physical p6**, gives the robust Bellman recursion under that model. **Corollary 1 and Theorem 2 (Markov optimality), physical p7**, allow deterministic Markov policies and make the robust value a function of the current state alone; p8 completes the induction. These statements are source facts, not newly verified proofs. Exact excerpts: p4 `Assumption 1 (Rectangularity)`; p7 `the robust value function` and `is a function of the current state`.

Mapping: our current measured state need not contain the predictive information in a correlated complete path. We cannot infer IY's rectangularity merely from linear dynamics, a shared box or fixed recourse. Replacing W by products of marginal disturbances changes the approved input. IY's discrete action setting also does not directly supply the continuous rational-box claim. Fixing the first program and asking Boolean feasibility at every rational q is not identical to optimizing an expected reward. This mismatch does not establish novelty.

## WKR: the relevant result is already history-sensitive

Physical pp1–2 identify the manuscript and define fully observed finite-state/finite-action MDPs, general randomized policies on state-action histories and discounted expected reward. Physical pp5–6 define affine transition kernels indexed by a bounded halfspace/ellipsoid parameter set with a Slater point. Physical p6 distinguishes `(s,a)`-rectangular from `s`-rectangular ambiguity: these are products of transition-kernel marginals, not products of deterministic disturbance coordinates.

**Proposition 2.3, physical pp8–9**, gives an optimal stationary policy for convex s-rectangular ambiguity of the declared form, and allows the necessity of randomization. **Proposition 2.4 (General Ambiguity Sets), physical p10**, explicitly states that non-rectangular policy improvement can require non-Markovian policies. Its construction reaches the same state 4 through state 2 or state 3 and chooses different actions according to that past state. The actual page was additionally rendered and visually read (`wkr-p10.png`); it is a proposition, not a mislabelled theorem. **Theorem 2.6, physical p11**, is a hardness-of-approximation result for worst expected reward with fixed stationary policies and the specified ambiguity family. Only its statement and the beginning of its proof on p12 were inspected; no reproduction of the reduction is claimed.

Mapping: history can help under coupled uncertainty is already in an inspected original. However, WKR's stored past state is genuinely available in its policy class; our closure has only x_r and the known first program/model. Neither its offline observation history for constructing ambiguity sets nor its online policy history can be borrowed as an extra sensor. Its expectation and transition-parameter ambiguity do not immediately imply an exact deterministic rational bound-feasibility theorem. Theorem 2.6 supplies no hardness claim about our single-closure residual LPs.

## DVM: an information state must be a function of information actually held

Physical p2 defines joint and conditional ranges on a sample space. Physical p3 specifies bounded sets, nonnegative bounded costs, Lipschitz/L-invertible observations, Lipschitz costs, independent primitive uncertain variables and perfect recall `M_t=(Y_0:t,U_0:t-1)`. Physical p4 **Definition 1** requires `Pi_t=sigma_t(M_t)`, equality of conditional worst costs for every memory/action, and sufficiency to predict its own conditional range. **Theorem 1, physical pp4–5**, concludes equality of the memory and compressed Q/V functions under these properties. Physical p5's partial-state example uses a conditional range given memory, with primitive independence explicitly restated. The sufficient state-like evolution/observation conditions are Lemma 1, not an automatic guarantee for an arbitrary statistic.

Correlated paths do not automatically make this source irrelevant: its input-output model allows observations and costs to depend on the entire primitive history. For a one-decision comparison, one may put the entire latent path in a single primitive variable W_0 taking values in the ACTUAL finite W and make the other primitives singleton-valued. This keeps correlations and does not reveal the path: the only observation remains `Y_0=z_a(p)`, and the action is the entire b. This is a modelling reduction to inspect, not a new theorem or permission to observe W_0. Under that representation the available memory at the decision is just the measured z and known constants, not p. The source's definition therefore cannot legitimate `rho_p` if p is unidentifiable.

The hard constraints and signed objective are not literally DVM's bounded nonnegative cost. For a fixed q a possible comparison encoding is the maximum of zero and all terminal hard/cost violations, a bounded nonnegative Lipschitz function on the fixed bounded data/action domains. Zero attained loss corresponds to feasibility. Infimum zero alone is not a rational feasible witness; minimum attainment and rational reconstruction would need explicit justification before invoking any theorem as an exact campaign result. No such proof work was undertaken. A shift of a bounded signed cost alone does not encode hard feasibility. Uniformity over q or arbitrary losses must not be silently added to a fixed-loss theorem.

## Two target selections and scoped closure

1. **W2-S1: prefix-conditional support as an implementable augmentation.** Proposed conclusion: for fixed a and terminal task, observing `(z_a(p),rho_p)` preserves every prefix-feasible rational bound. Rejected as an actionable target: rho_p is not generally available from the approved input. If it factors through z it cannot add information; even then it does not show that incompatible prefix continuations can be implemented. If it does not factor, it adds an unauthorized sensor. IY/WKR do not repair this, and DVM explicitly requires an available-memory function. No useful non-elementary delta was established.
2. **W2-S2: use observation-conditioned support instead.** Define `rho_bar(z)` from the union of actual suffixes over all prefixes producing z. It is computable from the supplied finite W and known model/a, and its rowwise inequalities describe the already inherited common-continuation system. Rejected as a contribution: this is state-cell robustification/residual reconstruction, with no recovery of the stronger prefix oracle in general. DVM's available-memory requirement explains the distinction. A smaller task-specific statistic may exist, but changing its representation without a new mathematical mechanism is not an established research target.

No proof or new falsification experiment follows. The exact availability test, quantifiers, rejected implications and evidence still required are in `availability.md` and `claim-contract.json`. No third selection was made. Source mismatch, known reconstruction and search silence are not novelty evidence.
